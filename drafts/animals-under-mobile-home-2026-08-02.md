## Why the Space Under Your Mobile Home Is Prime Real Estate for Critters

Think about what an animal wants in a Florida summer: shade, dry ground, protection from hawks and coyotes, and soft material for a nest. The crawl space under a mobile home offers all four, plus it stays 10 to 15 degrees cooler than the open yard in July.

- **Rats and mice**, which chew wiring, ductwork, and water lines
- **Snakes**, which follow the rodents in

Every one of these animals got in through an opening you can find and close. Damaged skirting is the number one entry point we see. A single missing or [buckled skirting panel](/blog/mobile-home-skirting-buckling/) is an open door with a welcome mat.

## Step One: Get the Animals Out Before You Seal Anything

This is the step people skip, and it's the one that causes the most expensive mistakes. If you seal up your skirting with an animal still under the home, one of two things happens. Either it dies under there and you're living with the smell for weeks, or it tears a new hole to get out and you're back where you started.

Here's the safe eviction sequence:

1. **Confirm what you're dealing with.** Sprinkle a band of flour or fine sand near the suspected entry point in the evening. Check for tracks in the morning. Raccoon prints look like tiny hands, armadillo tracks show claw drag marks, and cats leave round prints with no claws.
2. **Check for babies.** From February through early summer, assume any raccoon or opossum under your home is a mother with young. Sealing her out while babies are inside is how you end up with dead animals in your insulation.
3. **Make the space unpleasant.** A work light left on under the home, a radio playing talk stations, and ammonia-soaked rags near the nest area will often convince a mother to move her litter on her own within a few nights.
4. **Install a one-way door.** For stubborn animals, a wire one-way exit over the main entry lets them leave but not return. Give it three to five quiet nights before you consider the space empty.
5. **Verify it's vacant.** Repeat the flour test. No fresh tracks for three straight nights means you're clear to seal.

A word of caution on raccoons specifically. Raccoon droppings can carry roundworm eggs that are dangerous to people and pets, so don't handle a latrine site bare-handed or stir up dry droppings. The [CDC's guidance on Baylisascaris](https://www.cdc.gov/baylisascaris/about/index.html) explains the cleanup precautions. If there's a large latrine under your home, that's a job for someone in protective gear.

Also know that Florida law limits where you can relocate trapped wildlife. If eviction tactics fail, hire a licensed nuisance wildlife trapper rather than driving a raccoon across the county yourself.

## How to Keep Animals Out From Under a Mobile Home: The Five-Step Seal-Up

Once the crawl space is empty, you have a short window to close every opening before the next tenant finds them. Work through these five steps in order.

**1. Walk the full perimeter twice.** Once standing, once crouched at skirting level with a flashlight. Mark every gap, crack, loose panel, and burrow with spray paint or flags. Animals only need an opening of a few inches, so be picky. Our [underside inspection checklist](/blog/mobile-home-underside-inspection-checklist/) walks through exactly what to look for panel by panel.

**2. Repair or replace damaged skirting.** Snap loose vinyl panels back into their track and replace anything cracked, chewed, or missing. If more than a quarter of your skirting is failing, replacement usually beats patching. Solid options like foam-backed vinyl or faux rock hold up to animal pressure far better than thin bowed panels. Our [mobile home skirting service in Ocala](/mobile-home-skirting-ocala/) includes animal-resistant reinforcement at ground level.

**3. Bury a dig barrier where diggers are active.** Skirting alone won't stop an armadillo. Trench down 6 to 12 inches along problem sections and install galvanized hardware cloth in an L shape, with the foot of the L angled outward. When the animal digs at the base and hits wire that extends toward it, it gives up. Use half-inch mesh, not chicken wire, which rats pass through easily.

**4. Seal utility penetrations.** Water lines, the AC line set, dryer vents, and electrical conduit all pass through the skirting or belly. Pack gaps around pipes with steel wool or copper mesh, then cover with hardware cloth or metal flashing. Foam alone doesn't work because rodents chew straight through it.

**5. Fix the moisture that attracts them.** Animals seek water as much as shelter. A dripping drain line or [standing water under your mobile home](/blog/water-under-mobile-home/) is a magnet for everything from rats to snakes. Repair leaks, regrade so rain drains away from the skirting, and make sure your [vapor barrier](/vapor-barrier-ocala/) is intact so the ground under the home stays dry.

## Keep Them From Coming Back: A Simple Maintenance Routine

Sealing the crawl space gets you to zero. A little routine keeps you there.

**Do a perimeter walk every season.** Four times a year, walk the skirting line and look for new gaps, fresh digging, chew marks, and panels pulling out of their track. Ten minutes, tops. After any tropical storm or hard freeze, walk it again since skirting takes a beating in weather.

**Cut off the food supply.** Feed pets indoors or pick up bowls after meals. Keep trash in cans with locking lids. Move bird feeders well away from the home, because spilled seed is a rodent buffet that leads them straight to your skirting. The University of Florida's IFAS Extension has a solid rundown on [managing nuisance wildlife around Florida homes](https://edis.ifas.ufl.edu/entity/topic/nuisance_wildlife) if you want to go deeper.

**Trim the landing zones.** Keep shrubs and grass trimmed back a foot or two from the skirting so you can see digging early, and cut tree branches that overhang the roof. Rats and raccoons use branches as highways.

**Check the vents, don't block them.** Your skirting needs ventilation to prevent moisture buildup, so never seal vents shut to keep animals out. Instead, back each vent opening with half-inch hardware cloth. You keep the airflow and lose the entry point.

**Look under the home once a year.** Pop a panel, run a flashlight across the belly, and look for hanging insulation, droppings, or daylight where there shouldn't be any. Small tears in the belly board are a $250 fix now and a $2,500 fix after a family of raccoons finds them.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What smells keep animals out from under a mobile home?

Ammonia-soaked rags, predator urine granules, and used cat litter near entry points can encourage animals to leave a space they're already using. But no smell works as a long-term barrier, because animals get used to odors and rain washes them away. Treat repellents as an eviction tool, then rely on physical sealing to keep the space closed.

### Can animals under a mobile home cause structural damage?

Yes, in two main ways. Armadillo and rodent burrows loosen the soil under pier footings, which lets piers settle and puts the home out of level. Raccoons and rats also tear open the belly board, which lets moisture reach the insulation and subfloor and can lead to soft, sagging floors over time.

### Is it legal to trap and relocate wildlife in Florida?

Florida has strict rules on trapping and relocating nuisance wildlife, and in most cases relocating an animal off your property requires meeting state conditions or using a licensed trapper. Raccoons in particular usually can't just be released elsewhere. When eviction tactics fail, hiring a licensed nuisance wildlife trapper is the safe and legal route.

### Will sealing my skirting cause moisture problems underneath?

Not if you keep the ventilation your skirting was designed with. The goal is to block animal-sized openings, not airflow, so cover vents with half-inch hardware cloth instead of sealing them shut. Pair that with an intact vapor barrier on the ground and the crawl space stays dry, ventilated, and animal-free at the same time.
