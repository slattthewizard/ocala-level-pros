# Mobile Home Skirting Options: Vinyl, Metal, Brick, and Concrete Compared

Would you leave the bottom three feet of your house wide open to rain, raccoons, and 95 degree humidity? That's exactly what a mobile home without good skirting does every single day, and here in Marion County the underside of your home takes a beating for it.

The good news is you've got real choices. The four main mobile home skirting options are vinyl, metal, brick, and concrete, and they range from a few hundred dollars in materials to a five figure masonry project. Each one handles Florida heat, moisture, and the occasional hurricane season differently. Pick right and you protect your piers, your plumbing, and your insurance standing. Pick wrong and you're back out there in two summers, pulling warped panels off the ground.

We install and repair skirting all over Ocala, Belleview, Summerfield, Dunnellon, and the rest of Marion County, so we've seen every one of these materials succeed and fail up close. Here's the honest breakdown.

## Why Your Skirting Choice Matters More Than Looks

Skirting isn't just trim. It's the barrier between your home's underside and everything Florida throws at it.

Good skirting keeps rain splash and standing water away from your piers and pads. It blocks armadillos, possums, and stray cats from setting up camp under your floor. It also shades your belly board and ductwork from direct sun, which helps your AC and keeps your [vapor barrier](/vapor-barrier-ocala/) doing its job instead of baking.

There's a paperwork angle too. When a 4-point inspection or a pre-closing walkthrough happens, missing or collapsed skirting is one of the first things an inspector photographs. It signals deferred maintenance, and it gives them a clear view of any pier or tie-down issues underneath. Solid, vented skirting makes that whole process go smoother.

One more thing most homeowners don't realize: skirting problems are often a symptom, not the disease. When panels buckle or pull away from the track, the home itself may have settled and shifted. We wrote a whole piece on [why skirting buckles](/blog/mobile-home-skirting-buckling/), and the short version is this. If your skirting is wavy, check your level before you buy new panels.

## Vinyl Skirting: The Budget Favorite

Vinyl is the most common skirting in Marion County parks and on private lots, and there's a reason. It's cheap, light, and fast to install. A typical single wide takes about 160 to 200 linear feet of skirting, and vinyl materials for that run roughly $600 to $1,400 depending on panel quality. Installed by a pro, most single wides land between $1,200 and $2,800.

The pros are easy to list. Vinyl never rusts, it comes in colors that match most siding, panels snap in and out for underside access, and repairs are simple. If a weed trimmer chews up one panel, you replace one panel, not a wall.

The cons are just as real. Thin builder-grade vinyl gets brittle in Florida sun and can crack after 8 to 12 years. It's the material most likely to blow out in a strong storm, and it offers zero impact resistance. A riding mower, a dog, or a determined armadillo can all punch through it.

Marlene in Belleview learned the panel-quality lesson the hard way. She'd paid $900 for bargain vinyl skirting on her single wide, and after three summers the south side had faded, warped, and cracked at the ground track. We replaced it with heavier gauge vinyl for $2,100 installed, added proper venting, and that side of her home has held flat since. Cheap vinyl isn't always cheap once you buy it twice.

Our take: vinyl is a fine choice for most homes if you buy mid-grade or better panels and get the top and bottom tracks anchored correctly. Skip the thinnest product on the shelf.

## Metal Skirting Options for Mobile Homes

Metal skirting usually means ribbed steel or aluminum panels, similar to what you'd see on a pole barn. For a single wide, materials generally run $1,000 to $2,500, with installed prices between $2,000 and $4,500.

Metal's big win is toughness. It shrugs off weed trimmers, mower debris, pets, and wildlife. It doesn't get brittle in the sun, and properly fastened steel panels handle wind far better than vinyl. For homeowners near open pasture in Citra or out toward Dunnellon, where animals under the home are a constant fight, metal is often the smartest mid-priced option. We cover the critter problem in detail in our guide to [animals under a mobile home](/blog/animals-under-mobile-home/).

The tradeoffs: steel can rust at cut edges and at ground contact, especially in low spots where water collects. Metal also dents, and unlike vinyl, a dented panel stays dented. Ventilation has to be planned in with cut vents or vented panels, because metal is otherwise airtight, and a sealed crawl space in Florida humidity is a mold factory. The University of Florida's IFAS Extension publishes solid guidance on moisture control in Florida homes if you want the science behind that at [edis.ifas.ufl.edu](https://edis.ifas.ufl.edu/).

Ray in Summerfield went with steel after losing vinyl panels in two straight storm seasons. His double wide took about 240 linear feet, and the installed price was $3,800. That was almost double his vinyl quote, but he's now been through several tropical systems without losing a panel, and he stopped patching armadillo holes entirely.

## Brick and Concrete Skirting: The Permanent Options

Brick and concrete are in a different league, both in performance and price.

Brick skirting means a real masonry curtain wall laid on a poured footer around the home's perimeter. It looks like a site-built house, it lasts 50 years or more, and it's essentially animal-proof and stormproof. Expect $6,000 to $15,000 or more for a typical home in our area, depending on footer requirements, height, and brick choice.

Concrete options come in two flavors. Poured or block perimeter walls perform like brick at a similar or slightly lower cost, usually $5,000 to $12,000. Faux concrete or cement board panels are the budget version. They're fiber cement or foam-backed panels that look like stone or stucco, run about $3,000 to $6,500 installed, and give you much better durability than vinyl without a masonry budget.

Two cautions before you fall in love with masonry. First, a rigid wall around a home that still moves is a bad combination. Mobile homes settle, and in Marion County's sandy soil they settle unevenly. If the frame drops even half an inch after the wall goes up, you can end up with cracked mortar and a skirting wall carrying weight it was never meant to carry. Get the home professionally leveled first. Our page on [mobile home leveling in Ocala](/mobile-home-leveling-ocala/) explains what that involves.

Second, permanent perimeter walls can change how your home is classified for lending and inspections. HUD sets the federal standards for manufactured home foundations and installations, and it's worth skimming their manufactured housing resources at [hud.gov](https://www.hud.gov/program_offices/housing/rmra/mhs/mhshome) before you commit to a permanent enclosure, especially if you might sell or refinance.

Dale and Carla in Silver Springs planned to stay in their double wide for good, so they budgeted for block. We releveled the home first for $1,400, they had a mason pour the footer and lay the wall for right around $9,500, and the result looks like a site-built ranch. Their skirting decision was really a foundation decision, and they treated it that way.

## Ventilation, Access, and Florida Rules You Can't Skip

Whatever material you choose, three details make or break the install.

Ventilation comes first. Florida humidity means the space under your home needs airflow, generally about one square foot of net vent area for every 150 square feet of crawl space, with vents placed near corners for cross-breeze. Skip the vents and you'll grow condensation on your belly board, rust your anchors, and feed mold. If you've ever smelled that musty odor coming up through the floor, poor skirting ventilation is a usual suspect. Standing water makes it worse fast, and our article on [water under a mobile home](/blog/water-under-mobile-home/) covers what to do when you find it.

Access comes second. Code and common sense both call for at least one access panel, usually 18 by 24 inches or larger, so plumbers, inspectors, and yes, leveling crews can get under the home. Masonry walls need a framed access door built in from day one. Cutting one in later is expensive and ugly.

Ground contact is third. Vinyl and metal tracks should sit on grade that slopes away from the home. Burying panel bottoms in dirt traps moisture against the material and shortens its life. With masonry, the footer handles this, which is part of what you're paying for.

One Florida-specific note: skirting is not structural support and it's not an anchoring system. Your home still stands on its piers and holds down with its tie-downs. If those are in rough shape, new skirting just hides the problem. ## How to Choose the Right Skirting for Your Situation

Here's the quick decision path we walk homeowners through.

If you're in a park or plan to move or sell within five years, mid-grade vinyl is usually the right call. It's affordable, it looks clean, and buyers expect it.

If you're on acreage, fight wildlife, or have lost skirting panels in storms before, step up to metal or cement board panels. The extra $1,000 to $2,500 buys years of not dealing with it.

If this is your forever home and the home sits level and stable, brick or block is a genuine upgrade that changes how the whole property reads. Just level first, wall second. Always in that order.

And in every case, look under the home before you spend a dime on skirting. Sagging panels, doors that stick, and floors that slope are signs the piers underneath need attention. Our guide on the [signs a mobile home needs leveling](/blog/signs-mobile-home-needs-leveling/) lists the red flags in plain English. New skirting over an unlevel home is like painting over a leak.

When to stop DIY and call a pro: vinyl repairs and single panel swaps are fair game for a handy homeowner. Full installs on an out-of-level home, any masonry work, anything involving pier or tie-down problems, or crawling under a home that shows signs of shifting are not. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the cheapest skirting option for a mobile home?

Vinyl is the cheapest, with DIY material kits starting around $600 for a single wide and professional installs from about $1,200. Just avoid the thinnest bargain panels, because Florida sun makes them brittle within a few years. Mid-grade vinyl costs a little more up front and usually lasts twice as long.

### What's the best mobile home skirting for Florida weather?

For most Marion County homes, heavy-gauge vinyl or metal hits the sweet spot of storm performance and price, and masonry is the most durable option of all if the home is level and you're staying long term. Whatever you pick, proper venting matters as much as the material in our humidity. A sealed crawl space grows mold no matter what the panels are made of.

### Does skirting count as structural support for a mobile home?

No. Skirting is a weather and pest barrier, not a foundation. Your home is supported by its piers and held down by its tie-down system, and skirting should never carry the home's weight. If your skirting is bulging or cracking, that often means the home has settled and needs leveling, not just new panels.

### Should I level my mobile home before installing new skirting?

Yes, and this is the step most people skip. Skirting is cut and fitted to the home's current height, so if the home settles afterward, panels buckle, gaps open, and masonry can crack. A leveling check costs far less than redoing a skirting job, and it protects the investment you're about to make.
