## What Mobile Home Piers Do, and Why Florida Wears Them Out

Your mobile home sits on a steel frame, and that frame rests on a grid of piers. Most homes around Ocala sit on stacked concrete blocks with a poured pad or ABS plastic pad underneath, capped with wood or plastic shims that fine-tune the height. A typical single-wide has 12 to 20 piers. A double-wide can have 30 or more, including piers along the marriage line where the two halves join.

Each pier carries somewhere between 2,000 and 6,000 pounds. That's a lot to ask of stacked blocks sitting on Central Florida sand.

Our soil is the real enemy. Marion County sits on sandy soil over limestone, and that sand shifts when it gets soaked and dries out again. Summer thunderstorms dump water under homes with damaged skirting or no vapor barrier, the sand compacts unevenly, and piers start to sink, lean, or crack. We wrote more about that cycle in [why mobile homes settle in Florida](/blog/why-mobile-homes-settle-florida/).

Age matters too. Concrete blocks are rated for decades of service, but constant moisture, soil movement, and point loading crack them early. A pier that went in when the home was set in 1985 has been through 40 years of wet seasons.

## When Releveling Is Still the Right Call

Don't let anyone talk you into full pier replacement before you understand what releveling can fix. A proper relevel involves jacking the frame, adjusting or replacing shims, and sometimes re-stacking a pier or two. It handles a surprising amount.

Releveling usually solves the problem when:

- **Piers have settled but the blocks are intact.** Sinking is a soil problem, not a pier problem. Jacking and reshimming restores the height.
- **Shims are crushed, split, or missing.** Wood shims compress over the years. Replacing them is routine work.
- **Only a few piers are slightly out of plumb.** A pier leaning a degree or two can often be re-stacked square during the relevel.
- **Your symptoms are doors sticking, minor floor slopes, or small drywall cracks.** These usually trace back to settling, not structural pier failure. Our post on the [signs your mobile home needs leveling](/blog/signs-mobile-home-needs-leveling/) covers the full list.

## When to Replace Mobile Home Piers: 7 Signs They're Done

Some pier conditions can't be shimmed away. When we see these under a home, replacement goes in the estimate, because jacking a home onto a failing pier just transfers thousands of pounds onto a component that's already losing the fight.

**1. Cracked or split blocks.** Hairline surface cracks are common and usually cosmetic. Cracks that run through the block, spall off chunks, or let the block shift under hand pressure mean the pier has lost load capacity. Replace it.

**2. Piers leaning more than about 1 inch out of plumb.** A badly leaning pier isn't carrying load straight down anymore. It's on its way to kicking out entirely, and re-stacking the same crumbling blocks doesn't fix why it leaned.

**3. Crushed or rotted caps and shims that keep failing.** If a pier eats a new set of shims every year or two, the pier or the pad under it is the problem, not the shims.

**4. Sunken or broken pads.** The pad spreads the load into the soil. A cracked pad, or one that's punched down into the sand, lets the whole stack settle no matter how many times you relevel. This shows up a lot after tropical systems, which is why [settling after heavy rain](/blog/mobile-home-settling-after-rain/) is worth watching.

**5. Piers separated from the frame.** If you can slide a hand between the pier cap and the steel beam, that pier is carrying nothing. Its share of the load moved to its neighbors, which are now overloaded and wearing out faster.

**6. Mortar joints failing on mortared piers.** Some older setups used mortared block. Once the joints crumble, the stack acts like loose blocks with extra weight and less flexibility.

**7. The wrong pier for the load.** Single-stacked blocks under a marriage line, undersized piers under a later addition, or dry-stacked blocks taller than code allows without proper reinforcement. HUD's installation standards spell out pier requirements by height and load, and you can read them straight from the source at [HUD's Manufactured Housing Program](https://www.hud.gov/program_offices/housing/rmra/mhs/mhshome).

One or two bad piers doesn't mean the whole grid is shot. Most replacement jobs we do in Marion County swap 3 to 8 piers and relevel the rest. Full pier system replacement is rare and usually limited to homes from the 1970s that were set on undersized stacks to begin with. ## How Pros Decide Whether to Replace Mobile Home Piers or Reshim

When we inspect under a home, we're not eyeballing it and guessing. Here's the actual process, so you can judge whether any contractor's recommendation holds up.

We check the level first with a water level or laser across the frame, which tells us how far out the home is and where. Then we inspect every pier individually: block condition, plumb, cap and shim condition, pad condition, and contact with the frame. We probe the soil at suspect piers, because a pier sinking into a soft pocket needs a bigger pad or a poured footer, not just new blocks.

We also look at the pattern. Ten piers all settled evenly points to soil, so relevel. Three piers cracked on the wet side of the home points to a water problem, so we'll replace those piers and tell you to fix the gutter or grading feeding them. Piers failing under the marriage line of a double-wide gets extra attention because that's the highest load in the home.

The moisture question matters more than most owners realize. Standing water under a home destroys piers faster than anything else, and it usually comes with a shredded vapor barrier. If that's part of your problem, pairing pier work with a new [vapor barrier](/vapor-barrier-ocala/) protects the investment. Otherwise you're putting new piers into the same swamp that killed the old ones.

## What Pier Replacement Day Actually Looks Like

Replacement is less dramatic than it sounds. Your home doesn't get lifted into the air, and you don't need to move out.

The crew jacks the frame just enough to unload the failed pier, usually less than an inch. The old blocks and pad come out. A new pad goes down on compacted soil, new blocks get stacked and capped, shims are set, and the jack comes down so the pier takes its load. Then the next pier. ## Where DIY Ends and the Pros Begin

You can and should do your own quick checks. Walk the perimeter, look for leaning piers through skirting vents, and note doors that stick or floors that slope. Crawling under with a flashlight to photograph pier condition is fine if the home is stable and you don't touch anything.

Stop there. Jacking a mobile home puts tons of steel and lumber in motion over your body. Bottle jacks kick out, blocks shift, and frames drop without warning. Homes have killed people this way. Pier replacement also affects your tie-down system, since anchors and straps are set relative to the frame height, and Florida wind zone rules leave no room for improvisation. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long do mobile home piers last?

Concrete block piers routinely last 30 to 50 years when the soil is stable and the underside stays dry. In Central Florida's sandy, wet conditions, expect trouble spots sooner, especially on homes without a working vapor barrier. Individual pier failures in the 15-to-25-year range are common here, which is why periodic inspection matters more than the calendar.

### Can I replace just one or two bad piers?

Yes, and that's the most common repair we do. Isolated replacements run $150 to $350 per pier, and we relevel the surrounding area so the new pier carries its fair share. Replacing only the failed piers is completely legitimate as long as the rest of the grid checks out sound.

### Will homeowners insurance pay for pier replacement?

Usually not for wear, settling, or age, which insurers treat as maintenance. Damage from a covered event like a hurricane may qualify, so document conditions with photos before and after storms. Where piers really intersect with insurance is the 4-point inspection, since visible pier failure can hold up coverage or a sale.

### How do I know if my piers failed or the home just settled?

Settling shows up as a home that's out of level while the piers themselves look intact and plumb. Pier failure shows up as cracked or leaning block stacks, crushed caps, or gaps between pier and frame. From inside the home the symptoms feel identical, so the only reliable answer comes from a proper under-home inspection, which we do free anywhere in Marion, Sumter, and Citrus counties.
