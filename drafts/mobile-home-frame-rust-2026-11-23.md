# Mobile Home Frame Rust: What's Normal and What Needs Attention

Some rust on a mobile home frame is normal. A light orange film on the steel I-beams is surface rust, and in Florida it is common on older homes. What needs attention is rust that flakes in layers, leaves pits you can catch a fingernail in, or has eaten into the steel at a pier or strap connection. That is real section loss, and a structural repair is a welder's or engineer's job.

We don't weld, and we don't repair frames. Our work is under homes in Marion County, though, checking piers, anchors and the ground barrier, and the frame is right there. This post helps you tell the harmless kind from the kind that needs a professional, and it covers what causes it and what you can do about the conditions behind it.

## What the Frame Is

A manufactured home sits on a steel chassis, usually two long I-beams that run the length of the home. Cross members tie them together, and on many homes outriggers stick out from the main beams to carry the floor edges. The piers hold the home up by pushing against those beams. The tie-down straps wrap around or connect to them to hold the home down.

So the frame carries the weight of the home and takes the pull from the straps. That is why where the rust is matters as much as how much of it there is.

## Surface Rust: Usually Fine

Steel under a home sees damp air, sandy soil that holds moisture and Florida humidity. A film of orange or brown on the beams, with the metal still firm and square-edged underneath, is surface rust.

How to recognize it:

- Light orange or brown color, dusty or slightly rough to the touch
- No flaking, scaling or layers
- The edges of the steel are still sharp and the thickness looks the same
- A screwdriver tip scratches the surface and finds solid metal

Surface rust is a warning about moisture more than a structural problem. It tells you that damp air is reaching the frame, which is worth fixing for the home's sake. You can wire-brush it and coat it with a rust-converting primer and a paint made for steel, if you can get at it safely. Many homes have a factory coating that wears off with age.

## Scaling and Section Loss: Needs Attention

Rust that is working into the steel looks different:

- **Scaling.** Layers or plates of rust that peel off in flakes, sometimes thick enough to chip off with a screwdriver.
- **Pitting.** Rough, cratered patches where metal is gone.
- **Thinned edges.** The flange of an I-beam looks ragged or paper-thin, or the web has holes.
- **Holes.** Daylight shows through the steel.
- **Bulging.** Rust building up between two pieces of steel can push them apart.

If you see any of these, you are looking at section loss. The steel is thinner than it was built to be, and it can carry less. A screwdriver that pokes into a beam is a bad sign. Take photos and call a welder or structural engineer for a look.

Never get under a home that is supported only by a jack. And do not cut, grind or weld on a frame yourself. A frame that looks solid can be thin in spots you can't see.

## The Spots That Matter Most

Rust in some places is worse than rust in others. These are the ones to check:

1. **Where the piers touch the frame.** Metal on metal, or metal on wood, traps water. The top of a pier is also where the home's weight is concentrated. Look at the beam above each pier, and look at any steel pier caps or shims.
2. **Where the straps connect.** A frame tie wraps around or bolts to the beam, and rust at that connection weakens the whole tie-down. Our guide to [rusted mobile home anchors](/blog/rusted-mobile-home-anchors/) covers the anchor and strap side, so we won't repeat it here. The frame side is yours to check too.
3. **Outriggers and cross members.** They sit lower and closer to the ground, and they tend to stay wet.
4. **Low spots in the frame.** Look near the ends of the beams and any place where water would drip and sit.
5. **Under bathrooms, kitchens and the water heater.** A slow plumbing leak can soak the same beam for years.

If rust is bad at a pier contact point, the repair may need to happen before the home is leveled or lifted. A crew should not lift a home by a section of frame that is too thin to take the load.

## What Causes Frame Rust Under a Mobile Home

Steel rusts when it stays wet and has air. Under a home, that comes from a handful of causes:

- **Wet ground.** Sandy soil drains fast, but a heavy summer rain leaves the surface wet, and low spots hold water.
- **A missing or torn ground barrier.** The plastic sheet on the ground keeps moisture from rising into the crawlspace. Without it, damp air comes up all year. See our guide to the [best vapor barrier for a mobile home](/blog/best-vapor-barrier-mobile-home/).
- **Leaks from above.** Roof, plumbing and window leaks drip down the belly and onto the frame.
- **Standing water.** Poor drainage around the home, a downspout dumping next to the skirting, or a yard that slopes toward the home.
- **Poor ventilation.** Blocked vents trap humid air. Our post on [mobile home skirting vents](/blog/mobile-home-skirting-vents/) explains how vents work.
- **Age.** A home that has been in the same place for decades has had decades of damp.

Salt air is not much of a factor in inland Marion County, but humidity and rain are. Rust here is usually a moisture problem, so moisture is where the fix starts.

## What to Do About It

Work from the cause toward the steel.

**Step 1: Dry the crawlspace.** Fix leaks. Move downspouts and regrade so water runs away from the home. Open blocked vents and make sure the skirting is not trapping water. Our post on [water under a mobile home](/blog/water-under-mobile-home/) covers drainage.

## When the Level Is Part of the Story

A frame that is out of level or sitting on a pier that has sunk can trap water in low spots along the beam, and it can load a rusted section more than it was meant to carry. If the home is out of level, doors stick or the floor feels uneven, the frame may be taking stress it should not.

## How Often to Check

Look at the frame once a year with a flashlight, and again after a long wet stretch or a storm. Check the beams, pier contacts and strap connections, and note any rust that has spread since last time. A photo each year makes changes easy to see. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is rust on my mobile home frame normal?

Light surface rust is normal, especially on older homes in Florida. If the steel is firm, the edges are square and a screwdriver only scratches the surface, it is usually a cosmetic issue and a sign of moisture. Flaking, pitting or thin edges are different and need a closer look from a professional.

### How do I know if the I-beam rust is serious?

Check for scaling, deep pits, ragged or thin flanges, holes and any spot where a screwdriver pokes in. Look hardest at pier contact points and strap connections. If you find section loss, take photos and call a welder or structural engineer.

### Can a rusted mobile home frame be repaired?

Often yes, but it is a job for a welder or engineer, not a leveling crew. They decide whether to patch, plate or replace the damaged steel. We don't weld. We can fix the moisture sources, the ground barrier and the tie-downs, and check the level once the frame is sound.

### What causes the frame to rust under a mobile home?

Moisture is the cause. Wet ground, a missing or torn ground barrier, plumbing or roof leaks, standing water and blocked vents all keep the steel damp. Dry out the crawlspace and fix the ground barrier, and the rust slows down.
