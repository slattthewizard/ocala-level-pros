# Mobile Home Dryer Vent: Why It Has to Exit Past the Skirting

A mobile home dryer vent has to carry exhaust all the way outside the home, past the skirting. It cannot stop under the floor. HUD's installation standards say a clothes dryer exhaust must go to the exterior of the home, beyond any perimeter skirting, and that it must not terminate under the home. A vent that dumps into the crawl space puts hot, wet, lint-filled air right where you least want it.

We do not do dryer, HVAC or appliance work. We do see the results of bad dryer venting from underneath, in damp belly insulation, lint on the ground barrier and mold on the framing. This post covers the rule, what a correct setup looks like, and how the skirting fits in.

## What the HUD Rule Says

Here is the text, section by section, from 24 CFR Part 3285.

- **Section 3285.503(d), clothes dryer exhaust.** The exhaust duct system must be completed in accordance with the appliance manufacturer instructions. It says the vents must exhaust to the exterior of the home, beyond any perimeter skirting installed around it. A note under it says the dryer exhaust system must not contain reverse slope or terminate under the home ([24 CFR 3285.503](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-24/section-3285.503)).
- **Section 3285.505(f), crawlspace ventilation.** Dryer vents and combustion air inlets must pass through the skirting to the outside ([24 CFR 3285.505](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-24/section-3285.505)).

So the duct does not end at the floor or at the belly. It runs to an outlet outside the home, and where there is skirting, the duct passes through it. The vent cap sits outside, in open air.

This is the federal installation standard for manufactured homes. If you are not sure how it applies to your home, ask your park, your insurer or a licensed HVAC or appliance contractor.

## Why It Matters Under a Mobile Home

A dryer pushes a lot of warm, wet air. A load of laundry can send a large amount of water vapor out the vent. Under a mobile home, that air has nowhere to go.

What happens when it ends up under the floor:

- **Humidity builds.** Skirting vents cannot move that much moisture out, especially in a Florida summer when the outside air is already damp.
- **Belly insulation gets wet.** Wet insulation holds water, sags and stops insulating. It also pulls down the belly wrap.
- **Mold and rot.** Floor joists, the belly board and the subfloor stay damp. Our post on [mold under a mobile home](/blog/mold-under-mobile-home/) covers what that looks like.
- **Lint.** Dryer lint is dry fuel for a fire. It also mats on the ground barrier and clogs skirting vent screens.
- **Pests.** Damp, warm, lint-covered space attracts rats, roaches and termites.

## Common Dryer Venting Mistakes

These are the usual problems:

1. **Duct ends under the floor.** The flex duct was run through the floor and left open, or the end fell off the vent and no one noticed.
2. **Vent dumps into the skirting.** The duct reaches the skirt but does not pass through it, so the exhaust blows against the back of a panel.
3. **Crushed or sagging duct.** Foil or vinyl duct sags between the home and the skirting, collects lint and water, and restricts flow. HUD's note says no reverse slope.
4. **Torn or disconnected joints.** A joint that comes apart under the home pours exhaust into the belly.
5. **No cap or a clogged cap.** Lint and nests block the outlet, and the air goes backward into the duct.
6. **Duct cut through a skirting vent.** The panel is a vent panel, and the duct blocks it, so the home loses ventilation.

None of these is a flooring or leveling problem. They are moisture problems that show up on our side of the job.

## How the Skirting Fits In

Skirting adds one more step to a dryer vent. With bare crawlspace and no skirt, the duct can simply end past the home's edge. Once there is skirting, the duct has to go through it.

Here is what that setup involves:

- **A dedicated opening.** A clean hole cut in the skirting panel, sized to the vent hood or the dryer vent kit, not a gap where the duct squeezes by.
- **A vent hood or outlet on the outside face.** The exhaust leaves through a cap that opens to the air, usually with a damper, not a bare tube.
- **A sound, sloped duct.** The duct runs under the home in a straight, supported line, with no low spots where water and lint collect, as the rule on reverse slope warns.
- **A panel that is not also a vent.** Skirting vents have to stay free for crawlspace airflow, so the dryer duct gets its own opening.

Not every home has the dryer on the same wall as the skirting access, and a long duct run has its own limits that come from the dryer manufacturer. That is an appliance question, so check with the dryer's installation instructions or a licensed contractor.

## What a Mobile Home Dryer Vent Kit Does

Dryer vent kits sold for mobile homes are built for this layout. They usually include a length of duct, a floor or wall connection, clamps, and an outlet hood. Some are made for use through the floor, and others for through the wall.

A few points on choosing and using one:

- Use the kind that matches your dryer's manual. Duct type and maximum length are the manufacturer's rules.
- Metal duct holds up better than thin foil or vinyl under a home, where it can be nibbled, crushed or caught on a pier.
- Support the duct so it does not sag, and keep joints tight.
- Make sure the outlet is outside the skirting, with a hood that opens and is easy to clean.

Installing the duct is an appliance or HVAC task. If you want it done, hire a licensed contractor. On our skirting jobs, we can leave a clean opening in the panel for the outlet.

## Dryer Lint and Moisture: Signs There Is a Problem

You do not have to go under the house to spot a vent problem. Watch for:

- A dryer that takes more than one cycle to dry a load, or a laundry room that feels humid.
- Lint or a damp film around the skirting near the dryer wall.
- A musty smell inside the home near the laundry closet or floor registers.
- Fog or condensation on the inside of nearby windows.
- Dark or wet staining on the belly wrap or insulation below the dryer.
- The hood outside has lint or is not opening.

If you see these, look under the home near the dryer. Stay out from under a home supported only by a jack, and do not crawl in if you see exposed wiring or smell gas. Our [underside inspection checklist](/blog/mobile-home-underside-inspection-checklist/) covers what else to look at.

## Fixing the Damage After a Bad Vent

If the vent has been dumping under the home, there are two jobs: fix the vent, and fix the damage.

- **Vent:** a licensed contractor routes the duct outside past the skirting.
- **Belly wrap and insulation:** wet or sagging sections need replacement. Belly damage that reached the boards is covered in our post on [mobile home belly board repair](/blog/mobile-home-belly-board-repair/).
- **Ground barrier:** lint and standing water on the ground barrier need cleanup, and torn plastic needs repair. A spot repair runs $400-$900 on our price sheet, and a full replacement runs $2,000-$4,000.
- **Skirting:** a damaged or blocked panel can be repaired or replaced. Skirting repairs run $250-$800.

Fixing the dryer vent alone does not dry out a wet belly, so check the underside after the duct is moved.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Where does a dryer vent have to go on a mobile home?

Outside the home, beyond the skirting. HUD's installation standards require a clothes dryer exhaust to exhaust to the exterior of the home, beyond any perimeter skirting, and not to terminate under the home. Where there is skirting, the duct passes through it to the outside.

### Can I vent my dryer under the mobile home?

No. Exhaust under the home puts moist, lint-filled air into the crawlspace, which soaks belly insulation, feeds mold and rot, and leaves lint where it can catch fire. The vent has to run all the way outside.

### Do you install dryer vents?

No. We do not do dryer, HVAC or appliance work, so call a licensed contractor for the duct. We repair the skirting, ground barrier and belly wrap that dryer exhaust tends to damage.

### Can a dryer vent go through a skirting vent panel?

It should not. Skirting vents are there for crawlspace airflow, so the dryer duct needs its own opening and an outside hood. Running it through a vent panel cuts down the ventilation the home needs.

### How do I know if my dryer is venting under my mobile home?

Look under the home near the dryer wall for a loose or open duct, lint on the ground, or damp insulation. Check the outside of the skirting for a vent hood, and if there is not one, the exhaust is likely going somewhere it should not.
