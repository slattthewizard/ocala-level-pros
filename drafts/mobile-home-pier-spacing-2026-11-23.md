# Mobile Home Pier Spacing and Pier Height: What the Rules Say

Under HUD's installation standards, piers that support the frame must sit no more than 24 inches from each end of the home and no more than 120 inches apart, center to center, under the main rails. That is the outer limit, not a target. Your home's own installation manual usually calls for tighter spacing, and the manual wins.

This post covers the federal numbers for pier spacing, how tall a block pier can be before the rules change, the minimum clearance under the frame, and why Marion County sand makes spacing and height something to check, not guess.

One note first. The standards we quote are the federal Model Installation Standards in 24 CFR Part 3285. Their stated scope is the initial installation of new manufactured homes. A home set years ago, or built before the 1976 HUD code, may have been blocked to a different rule, so treat these numbers as the modern benchmark and not a verdict on an older setup.

## How Far Apart Mobile Home Piers Go

Section [3285.310](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-24/section-3285.310) covers pier location and spacing. It says the right spacing depends on the home's dimensions, the live and dead loads, whether it is single or multi-section, the I-beam size, soil bearing capacity, footing size, and things like where the doors and other openings are.

Then it sets the hard limits for piers under the frame: no more than 24 inches from both ends and no more than 120 inches center to center under the main rails. It also lets piers shift up to 6 inches in either direction along the beam to clear plumbing, wiring, or equipment. Another note in that section says a single-stack concrete block pier must not carry more than 8,000 pounds.

The tables in [3285.303](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-24/section-3285.303) tie spacing to load. Table 1, for frame blocking only, lists pier spacings with the load each pier carries, which climbs as the spacing widens and as the roof live load goes up. Wider spacing means each pier carries more.

That is why a crew does not just space piers at the 120-inch maximum. Wider spacing loads each pier and footing harder, and soft ground feels that first. The home's installation manual sets the real spacing for your home and roof load, and a data plate on the home tells you the loads it was built for. ## Where Else Piers Are Required

The frame piers are only part of it. The standards also call for support at certain spots.

- **Perimeter supports at openings.** Under [3285.311](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-24/section-3285.311), perimeter piers go on both sides of exterior side wall doors, such as entry, patio, and sliding glass doors, and at any other side wall opening 48 inches or wider. They also go under load-bearing porch posts, factory-installed fireplaces, and fireplace stoves.
- **Mate-line supports.** In a double-wide, the center line where the two halves meet needs its own support. Section [3285.310](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-24/section-3285.310) refers to mate-line and column piers, and the manufacturer's instructions lay them out.
- **Corner piers.** The rules for height, covered below, single out corner piers.

If your home has a wide patio door or a big window and the pier next to it is missing or leaning, that is worth flagging.

## Mobile Home Pier Height: Single Block, Double Block, and Engineered

Pier height is where the rules change in steps. Under [3285.306](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-24/section-3285.306), block pier construction depends on how tall the pier is:

| Pier height | What the rule says |
|---|---|
| Less than 36 inches | Single open or closed-cell blocks, 8 x 8 x 16 inches, are allowed when the block's design capacity is not exceeded. Horizontal offset from top to bottom must not exceed one-half inch. |
| 36 to 67 inches | Double, interlocked blocks. This also applies to all corner piers over three blocks high. Offset from top to bottom must not exceed one inch. |
| Over 67 inches | Must be designed by a registered professional engineer or registered architect. |

For perimeter and mate-line piers and piers at exterior wall openings, [3285.307](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-24/section-3285.307) allows single block piers to a maximum height of 54 inches, installed with the long side parallel to the perimeter rail.

So the practical answer to "what is the maximum pier height for a mobile home" is that block piers get more demanding as they get taller, and anything over 67 inches needs an engineer's design. Metal piers follow their own listing. Under [3285.304](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-24/section-3285.304), manufactured piers are selected so the adjustable risers do not extend more than 2 inches when finally positioned. For the block basics, such as 8 x 8 x 16 inch load-bearing blocks, cells stacked vertically, and caps, see [3285.304](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-24/section-3285.304) and our post on [mobile home pier types](/blog/mobile-home-pier-types/).

## Minimum Clearance: How High Off the Ground Is Required

If you are asking how high a mobile home can be off the ground, the rule is about the minimum, not a maximum. [Section 3285.305](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-24/section-3285.305) says a minimum clearance of 12 inches must be maintained between the lowest member of the main frame (I-beam or channel beam) and the grade under all areas of the home.

Twelve inches is tight. It leaves little room to crawl, inspect, or fix anything under there. It also means soil that washes in or piles up can eat into that clearance, and in sandy ground it does. The standards do not give a maximum height for the home off the ground. The limit comes from the pier height rules above and from what the foundation was designed for. A tall home on tall block piers is still held to the 67-inch engineering threshold, and tall piers get more sensitive to sideways loads, which matters in a Florida windstorm. ## Why Pier Spacing Goes Wrong in Marion County

A pier that is spaced right on paper can still fail in Marion County sand. Spacing is only one input, and the footing under each pier is the other.

- Heavy summer rain can wash sand out from under pads, so a pier that was fine becomes a low spot.
- Widely spaced piers put more load on each pad, so settlement shows up sooner.
- Added load such as a new porch or a heavy addition sits outside what the frame spacing was designed for.
- Missing or shifted piers, often after storms or earlier repairs, leave a longer unsupported span in the beam.

When a home sags between piers, you get floors that dip, doors that stick, and cracks above openings. ## What To Do With These Numbers

Use them as a check, not a design.

1. Walk under the home with a flashlight and look at the beam. Count the piers on each rail.
2. Measure the gap between neighbors. Anything near or past 120 inches center to center, or an end pier more than 24 inches from the end, is worth a second look.
3. Check pier height. A tall block pier that is single stacked is a flag, and so is any pier leaning more than an inch.
4. Measure the clearance between the bottom of the I-beam and the ground. Under 12 inches needs attention.
5. Find the installation manual or data plate, because the manual's spacing for your home is the real number.

Do not move or add piers yourself while the weight is on them. If the home needs more support, that is a job with a jack, cribbing, and a footing.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How far apart should mobile home piers be?

HUD's installation standards say piers under the frame must be no more than 24 inches from both ends and no more than 120 inches center to center under the main rails. Your home's installation manual often calls for tighter spacing based on its loads, and the manual controls.

### What is the maximum height for a mobile home pier?

Block piers under 36 inches can be single block, piers from 36 to 67 inches need double interlocked blocks, and piers over 67 inches must be designed by an engineer or architect. Perimeter and mate-line piers made of single block are allowed up to 54 inches.

### How much clearance does a mobile home need under the frame?

HUD's installation standards require a minimum of 12 inches between the lowest member of the main frame and the grade, under all areas of the home. There is no maximum listed in that section.

### Can I add piers myself if my home is sagging?

We do not recommend it. The load has to be lifted off first, the footing has to suit the soil, and the pier has to match the home's design loads. A poorly placed pier can put weight in the wrong spot and crack the frame or floor.

### Do older mobile homes follow the same pier spacing rules?

Not necessarily. HUD's installation standards are written for the initial installation of new manufactured homes. An older home may have been set to a different rule, so have the piers inspected and compared to the manufacturer's manual if you can find it.
