# Mobile Home Ceiling Cracks and Walls Separating From the Ceiling

Most mobile home ceiling cracks fall into three groups: a taped seam that opened up, a crack caused by the home moving, or a stain and sag from a roof leak. They look alike from the floor and need different fixes. A gap between the wall and ceiling that keeps coming back after you patch it is the one that points to settling.

This guide helps you sort them. We level homes, so we can tell you when the movement is the cause. We do not do drywall, paneling, or roofing, and we will say where those trades take over.

## Why Mobile Home Ceilings Crack Differently

Many mobile home ceilings are built from panels or gypsum board attached to the roof trusses, with seams covered by tape, trim, or batten strips. Walls and ceiling are separate pieces joined at the top plate. That is a lot of seams, and every seam is a place to show movement.

The home also sits on piers, not a slab. It flexes a little as the weather changes, the ground shifts, and a pier pad settles. A site-built house on a continuous foundation moves as one block. Your home moves in sections, and the ceiling takes up the difference.

Our post on [cracks in mobile home walls](/blog/cracks-mobile-home-walls/) covers wall cracks in general and what the patterns mean. Here we stay on the ceiling and the line where walls meet it.

## Cosmetic Seams and Tape Cracks

Some ceiling cracks are not the home's fault.

- **A thin, straight line along a panel seam.** Panel joints shift a little with heat and humidity. Joint tape or compound cracks, usually in a straight line, and the line often reappears in the same place.
- **Hairline cracks that have not changed.** If the crack was there last year and is the same width, it has probably stopped moving.
- **A gap at a trim or batten strip.** Strips shrink and loosen with age. They pull away without anything under the home moving.
- **Nail or screw pops.** Small bumps or circles where a fastener backed out, often with a tiny crack around them.
- **Cracks right after a hot, dry stretch or a humid one.** Temperature and moisture swings move materials.

Cosmetic cracks are a repair job for a drywall or handyman crew: re-tape, compound, sand, and paint. If the crack is cosmetic, that will hold.

## Cracks and Gaps That Point to Settling

When the home settles, the frame twists or drops, and the ceiling is one of the first places to show it. These patterns are the ones that matter.

- **Cracks that grow or come back after patching.** A patch that reopens within weeks means the home is still moving.
- **A gap between the wall and ceiling that is wider at one end.** One wall pulling away from the ceiling along its whole length suggests that corner or that side of the home dropped.
- **Cracks running diagonally from door and window corners up toward the ceiling.** Openings are weak points, and they rack first.
- **Doors and windows that stick at the same time.** Our post on [mobile home doors that won't close](/blog/mobile-home-doors-wont-close/) explains what that combination means.
- **A crack down the center of a double-wide.** The seam where the two halves join can open when the sections settle unevenly. That one deserves a separate look, because lifting only one half makes it worse.
- **Floors that slope, bounce, or feel uneven.** Settling shows up in the floor and the ceiling together.
- **Cracks on one side of the home only.** Settling is rarely even.

Two or three of those signs together is a strong case for a level check before any patch work.

## Cracks That Are Really Roof Leaks

Water stains deserve their own category, because the fix is different and the damage keeps growing.

Look for:

- **Brown or yellow stains.** They often have a ring or darker edge, and they spread after rain.
- **A sagging or soft ceiling panel.** Wet insulation and board get heavy and drop.
- **Peeling paint or bubbling near the stain.**
- **Stains near vents, skylights, the roof seam, or along the ridge.** Those are common entry points.
- **Cracks with a damp or musty smell.**

A stain by itself does not prove a leak is active. Touch the spot after a rain. If it is damp, water is getting in.

A roof leak is a roofer's job, and we do not do roofing. Call one quickly. Water in a ceiling cavity soaks insulation, rots framing, and grows mold. On a double-wide, a leaking roof seam can also be a sign that the halves have moved apart. If the roof is fixed and the seam keeps opening, the level is the next thing to check.

## How to Tell Which One You Have

A few checks do most of the sorting.

1. **Mark and date the crack.** Use a pencil at each end of the crack and write the date. Check again in a month.
2. **Measure the width.** Put a coin or a tape measure next to the crack and take a photo. A crack that widens is moving.
3. **Check the pattern.** Is it a straight seam line, or does it wander and run from corners? Wandering cracks that start at openings point to racking.
4. **Test doors and windows.** Sticking ones near the crack add weight to the settling theory.
5. **Check the floor.** Set a marble or a level down. Our post on [how pros check if a mobile home is level](/blog/how-pros-check-mobile-home-level/) goes through the tools.
6. **Look for water signs after rain.** Stains, damp spots, or a smell mean call a roofer first.

If you can see the underside of the home from outside, check the skirting for bulges or gaps at the corners. A buckling skirt on the same side as the ceiling gap is another vote for settling.

## What HUD Says About Level

HUD's installation standards set a yardstick. [24 CFR 3285.6](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-24/section-3285.6) says a manufactured home will be considered adequately leveled if there is no more than 1/4 inch difference between adjacent pier supports (frame or perimeter) and the exterior doors and windows of the home do not bind and can be properly operated.

That is an installation standard. It does not describe your ceiling. But it gives you a number to compare against: if the piers are off by more than that and the doors bind, the cracks are probably tied to the level.

Patch a ceiling gap while the home is still out of level and the patch will tear. You pay twice, once for the patch and again after the next relevel, when the finish work gets disturbed. The better order is:

1. Find and fix the water source if there is one, which means a roofer or plumber first.

4. Then hire a drywall or finish crew to patch and paint.

The patch stays because nothing is pulling on it anymore.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why are there cracks in my mobile home ceiling?

Usually one of three reasons: a taped seam opened with temperature and humidity changes, the home settled and moved the frame, or water is getting in from the roof. Cracks that grow, come back after patching, or start at door and window corners point to settling.

### Why is my wall separating from the ceiling?

When one side or corner of the home drops, the wall can pull away from the ceiling along its length. If the gap is wider at one end and doors or windows near it stick, the home has probably settled there.

### Can I just patch the crack and repaint?

You can, and for a cosmetic seam it will hold. If the home is still out of level, the patch will usually reopen. We level homes and do not do drywall or roofing, so you will need another trade for the patch.

### Is a ceiling crack in a mobile home a sign of a roof leak?

Only if there is a stain, a damp spot, a sag, or a musty smell near it. Plain cracks without water signs are more often seams or settling. If you see water signs, call a roofer promptly.

### Will releveling stop the ceiling cracks?

If settling caused them, yes, it stops the movement that opens them, so a patch applied afterward is far more likely to last. It will not repair the cracks themselves.
