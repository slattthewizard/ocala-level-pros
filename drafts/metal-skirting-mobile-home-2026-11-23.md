# Metal Skirting for a Mobile Home: Pros, Cons, and Florida Problems

Metal skirting for a mobile home is tough against weed trimmers, wind, and animals, and it is rough on the one thing Florida throws at it every day: wet ground. It will not crack the way old vinyl does, but it can rust at the bottom, dent for good, and cut hands. It also needs vents and an access panel just like any other skirting.

We do not install metal skirting. We put in vinyl and rigid-foam, and we repair them. So this is a straight read on metal from a crew that works under Marion County homes, not a sales pitch. If you are leaning toward metal, here is what to check before you pay for it.

## What People Mean by Metal Skirting

The words get used loosely, so pin down what you are being quoted.

- **Galvanized steel.** Steel sheet with a zinc coating. This is the usual choice and the one most people mean. It is often sold in ribbed or corrugated panels.
- **Corrugated metal skirting.** The wavy or ribbed profile, the same look as a barn or shed roof. The ribs make a thin sheet stiffer.
- **Tin skirting.** Almost never actual tin. People call any thin sheet metal tin, usually galvanized steel. Ask what the metal and the coating are.
- **Aluminum.** Lighter, does not rust, and dents more easily than steel. Usually shows up as trim and as some panels.

Federal installation rules do not name metal, but they do set a floor for the coating. Skirting must be weather-resistant or have protection against weather deterioration at least equal to a coating of zinc on steel of not less than 0.30 ounces per square foot of surface coated ([24 CFR 3285.504](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-24/section-3285.504)). Ask the supplier for the coating spec in writing. Bare or thinly coated steel is not what that rule describes.

## The Case for Metal

Metal has real strengths, and they are worth listing fairly.

- **It shrugs off abuse.** A string trimmer that chews vinyl barely marks it. Thrown mower rocks bounce off.
- **Animals struggle with it.** A raccoon or armadillo that pushes through a weak vinyl panel has a harder time with sheet metal, as long as the vents are screened and the bottom edge is tight.
- **It does not go brittle in the sun.** The sun fades paint, but a metal panel does not crack across the face the way aged vinyl does.
- **It can handle wind well when it is fastened right.** A solid, well-attached panel is not a loose flap in a gust.
- **Panels are long and fast to hang.** Fewer seams than a vinyl system.

If your home sits on open ground where wildlife is a constant fight, those points are real.

## The Case Against Metal in Marion County

**Rust at the bottom.** Marion County sand holds water, and summer rain keeps the ground wet for weeks. Metal that touches wet sand or sits in a low spot will rust from the bottom up, and cut edges are the weak point because the coating is gone there. A zinc coating helps, but it does not last forever against constant moisture. Keep the bottom edge a couple of inches above the soil and regrade so water runs away from the wall.

**Dents do not recover.** Vinyl flexes and snaps back. A dented metal panel stays dented. A bumped mower or a fallen limb leaves a mark you will see for years.

**Sharp edges and cuts.** Cut sheet metal bites. If you plan to crawl through an access panel, or kids and pets play near the wall, the cut ends and screw tips need to be covered or folded.

**Heat.** Metal holds and radiates heat. A sunlit metal wall gets hot to the touch in July. It shades the space under the home like any skirting, but it also warms the air along that wall, and a dark color makes it worse. Light colors help.

**Fastening problems.** Metal needs the right fasteners. Screws that rust bleed streaks down the face and fail, and over-driven screws pull through the panel. Top attachment is the hard part. Federal rules say skirting must not be attached in a way that traps water between the siding and trim, or in a way that impedes the expansion and contraction of the home's exterior covering ([24 CFR 3285.504](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-24/section-3285.504)). A top trim fastened tight and sealed along its length can break both.

**Noise.** Wind and rain on a thin sheet rattle and drum. Some owners like it. Many do not.

**Electrical contact.** Metal skirting that touches the home or the home's wiring is a bad idea. Have an electrician check any metal that could touch a live part, since we do not do electrical work.

## Venting and Access Still Apply

Metal skirting is solid, so every vent and opening has to be planned in. It does not breathe on its own.

Federal installation rules require ventilation openings on a crawlspace with skirting. The minimum net area is one square foot for every 150 square feet of floor area, reducible to one square foot for every 1,500 square feet when a vapor retarder covers the ground under the whole home. Openings must be on at least two opposite sides, as high as practicable, with a rodent-proof, corrosion-resistant covering. An access opening at least 18 inches wide, 24 inches high, and three square feet in area must be located so utility connections under the home are reachable ([24 CFR 3285.505](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-24/section-3285.505)).

On metal, ask three questions. Are vents cut into the panels or are vented panels used? Are the edges of every cut finished so they do not rust? And does the access panel open and close without a tool and without fighting the ribs? ## Metal vs Vinyl Skirting

| | Metal | Vinyl |
|---|---|---|
| Impact and animals | Better | Weaker, can crack or be pushed through |
| Rust and wet ground | Weak point at the bottom edge | Does not rust |
| Sun and heat | Does not crack, but runs hot | Fades and can go brittle, expands in heat |
| Dents | Stay dented | Flex and recover |
| Repair | Replace a sheet, finish the cut edges | Swap one panel |
| Underside access | Panels often screwed on | Panels snap in and out |
| Look | Barn or shop look | Matches most siding |

For most Ocala homes in a park, vinyl is the practical choice because it is easy to repair and easy to remove when a plumber or a leveling crew needs underneath. For a rural lot with a wildlife problem, metal can make sense if you get the coating, the bottom clearance, and the vents right. ## What We Would Check Before You Buy

1. **The coating.** Ask for the zinc coating spec, not just a gauge.
2. **Bottom clearance.** The panel should end above the soil, not in it.
3. **Drainage.** Water should run away from the wall.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is metal skirting good for a mobile home in Florida?

It can be, mostly on rural lots where animals are a problem. It resists impact and does not crack in the sun. The downsides are rust at the bottom edge in wet sand, dents that do not recover, heat, and sharp cut edges. Vents and an access opening are still required.

### Does galvanized metal skirting rust?

Yes, eventually, especially at cut edges and where it touches wet soil. The zinc coating slows rust but does not stop it. Keep the bottom edge off the ground, regrade so water runs away, and finish every cut edge.

### Is metal skirting better than vinyl?

It is tougher against impact and animals, and it does not go brittle. Vinyl does not rust, is easier to repair, and is simpler to remove for underside access. Neither is better in every case, so it depends on the lot, the budget, and how much wet ground you have.

### Does metal skirting need vents?

Yes. Federal installation rules require ventilation openings sized by floor area, placed on at least two opposite sides, plus an access opening of at least 18 by 24 inches and three square feet. Solid metal does not breathe, so the vents have to be cut in or built in.
