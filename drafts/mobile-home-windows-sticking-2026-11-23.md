# Mobile Home Windows Sticking or Won't Open? Check the Level First

A mobile home window that sticks is usually one of two things: a dirty or painted-over track, or a frame that has gone out of square because the home settled. One stubborn window is almost always hardware. Several windows binding at once, especially on the same side of the home and with doors sticking too, point to the level.

This guide shows how to sort the two. It covers the quick fixes you can do in an afternoon, the pattern that means the home has moved, and what HUD's installation rule says about windows that bind. We handle the level, piers, and anchors. We do not replace or repair windows.

## Start With the Cheap Causes

Rule out the simple stuff first. It takes 20 minutes and spares you a service call.

- **Dirt and grit in the track.** Florida sand and pollen pack into window tracks. Vacuum the track, scrub it with an old toothbrush, and wipe it dry.
- **Paint or caulk bonded to the sash.** A fresh paint job or a bead of caulk that ran into the gap can glue a window shut. Run a utility knife along the seam to break the bond.
- **Dry, sticky, or corroded hardware.** Aluminum windows corrode in humid air. A silicone-based spray in the track and on the rollers often frees a sash. Skip oily sprays, which hold dust.
- **Worn rollers or balances.** Older sliding windows wear out their rollers. A sash that drags at the bottom or lifts unevenly points here.
- **Swollen trim or interior casing.** Heavy summer humidity swells wood and some trim. If the window frees up in winter and sticks in August, that may be all it is.

If cleaning and lubricating fixes it, you are done. A window repair or replacement crew can handle worn rollers, broken locks, and failed seals.

## Signs the Frame Is Out of Square

When the home settles, the walls and the openings in them rack. A rectangular window opening gets pulled into a parallelogram, and a rectangular window will not move freely in a parallelogram. That is a level problem, and no amount of track cleaning will fix it.

Look for these clues:

- **Uneven gaps around the sash.** The gap between the sash and frame is wider at one corner than the others, or tight on top and open at the bottom.
- **A sash that rubs at one corner only.** Scuff marks in one spot on the frame tell you where it binds.
- **Cracks at the window corners.** Diagonal cracks running from the corners of the window out into the wall are the classic sign. Our post on [cracks in mobile home walls](/blog/cracks-mobile-home-walls/) shows what the patterns mean.
- **Several windows sticking, not one.** If three windows won't budge and they are all on the same wall or the same end of the home, the problem is bigger than three tracks.
- **Doors sticking too.** Doors and windows rack together. If your doors also drag or won't latch, read our post on [mobile home doors that won't close](/blog/mobile-home-doors-wont-close/). We do not repeat it here, but the cause is the same.
- **New gaps around the frame.** Light, drafts, or a visible gap where the window meets the wall that was not there before.
- **Windows that stick worse after heavy rain.** Wet sandy soil softens the ground under a pier pad, and the home drops there.

A single sticking window with clean, even gaps and no cracks nearby is almost certainly hardware. Three or more of the signs above is a level check.

## What HUD Says About Level and Windows

HUD's installation standards link the two. [24 CFR 3285.6](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-24/section-3285.6) says a manufactured home must be adequately leveled so its performance is not adversely affected. It then says the home will be considered adequately leveled if there is no more than 1/4 inch difference between adjacent pier supports (frame or perimeter) and the exterior doors and windows of the home do not bind and can be properly operated.

The rule treats windows that bind as a sign the level is off. It is a federal installation standard, not a promise about what any one repair will do, but it is a good yardstick. If your windows and doors bind, the home does not meet that yardstick.

## A Simple Level Check You Can Do

You do not need a laser to get a read on the home.

1. **Test every window and door.** Open and close each one. Write down which ones bind and where they rub. Mark the corners on a sketch of the floor plan.
2. **Look for a pattern.** Sticking windows grouped on one wall or one corner of the home usually sit above the pier that dropped.
3. **Roll a marble or set a level on the floor.** Try several rooms. If the marble always heads the same way, the home tilts that way.
4. **Walk the outside.** Look at the skirting top line from across the yard. A wavy line or a gap opening at one corner means the home moved.
5. **Take photos.** Photograph the sash gap and any corner cracks, with a tape measure in the shot. A before-and-after record shows if it is getting worse.

For a closer look at how a pro measures, see [how pros check if a mobile home is level](/blog/how-pros-check-mobile-home-level/). Our post on [signs your mobile home needs leveling](/blog/signs-mobile-home-needs-leveling/) lists the other warning signs to compare against.

If you decide to look under the home, never get under a home supported only by a jack. Leave the lifting to a crew with proper support.

## Why Forcing the Window or Shaving the Frame Backfires

It is tempting to hit the sash with a mallet, plane the frame, or pry it free. Don't.

- **Forcing a racked window can crack the glass.** A frame under stress is holding the glass tight, and a hard shove can break it.
- **Trimming the sash makes a gap that lets in water and bugs.** When the home is later brought back to level, the trimmed window no longer fits.
- **Loosening the frame hides the problem.** A window that opens again may only mean the frame moved enough to free it, while the underlying settling continues.

The same logic applies to doors and cracks. Treat the sticking as a symptom and fix what is causing it.

## What the Fix Looks Like

If the windows are binding because of settling, the repair is a releveling job.

The crew measures the home to find which piers dropped and by how much. Then they lift the frame in small steps at the low piers and set shims or replace pier parts until the readings are back within HUD's 1/4 inch figure between adjacent piers. Then they test the doors and windows again. Windows that stuck because the opening was racked often free up once it is back to square.

Sometimes they don't. A window that has been forced, painted, or run on a bent frame for years may still need repair. That is a window crew's job, not ours, and the best order is level first, window work second.

While the crew is under the home, they look at the anchors and the ground barrier too, because a home that moved once can move again.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why won't my mobile home windows open?

Most often the track is packed with dirt or the sash is bonded by paint or caulk. If several windows bind at once, especially on one side of the home and along with sticking doors, the home has likely settled out of level and the openings have gone out of square.

### How do I know if it is the window or the home?

One sticking window with even gaps and no cracks nearby is usually the window. Several sticking windows, uneven gaps around the sash, cracks at the corners, and doors that stick too usually mean the home has moved.

### Will releveling fix sticking windows?

Often yes, if settling caused the binding. HUD's installation rule treats a home as adequately leveled when there is no more than 1/4 inch difference between adjacent pier supports and the exterior doors and windows do not bind. A window that has been forced or bent for years may still need work from a window contractor.

### Do you repair or replace mobile home windows?

No. We level homes, install tie-downs and anchors, do vapor barriers and skirting, and handle inspections. If the window itself is worn or broken, call a window contractor. If the home is out of level, call us first, because new windows in a racked opening will bind too.

### Why do my windows stick more in summer?

Humidity swells wood trim and some frames, and heavy summer rain can soften the ground under pier pads. The first is a seasonal nuisance. The second is a settling problem that needs a level check.
