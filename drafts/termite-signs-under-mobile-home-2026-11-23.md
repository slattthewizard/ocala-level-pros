# Termite Signs Under a Mobile Home in Florida

Termites in a mobile home usually show up under the floor first. The signs to look for are thin mud tubes running up piers and blocks, wood that sounds hollow or crumbles when you press it, and swarmer wings near a window or door. ## Why Termites Like Mobile Homes in Florida

Subterranean termites live in the soil and need moisture. They build mud tubes to travel from the ground to wood without being exposed to dry air. A mobile home gives them several things:

- **Damp soil under the home.** Sandy soil drains fast, but summer rain and leaking plumbing keep a crawlspace wet for long stretches.
- **Wood close to the ground.** Floor joists, shims, skirting framing and wooden piers sit a couple of feet above the dirt, and anything that touches soil is a shortcut.
- **Dark, undisturbed space.** Nobody looks under there, so a colony can work for a long time before anything shows up inside.
- **A warm climate.** There is no winter slowdown to speak of.

None of this means your home has termites. It means the conditions deserve a look.

## Mud Tubes on Piers and Blocks

Mud tubes are the clearest sign you can check yourself. They are narrow channels, usually about the width of a pencil, made of soil and termite saliva. They run up the face of concrete block piers, along the sides of steel stands and footings, and up the sides of anything else that connects the ground to wood.

How to read what you find:

- Tubes running up the outside of a pier toward the beam or floor: possible termite travel.
- Tubes on the underside of the floor or belly board: worth a call, because that is higher than the usual path.
- A tube that crumbles when you break it and is empty inside may be an old one. A damp tube with live insects inside is active.
- Do not sweep the tubes away before the inspector sees them. They show where termites are traveling and help the pest control company find the entry.

Shine a flashlight, look, and photograph what you find. You do not need to touch anything.

## Wood in Contact With Soil

Anything wooden that touches the ground under a mobile home is a risk. The usual suspects:

- **Wood shims and blocking.** Old wood shims and stacked wood blocks used to level a home sometimes sit directly on soil or on a pier pad that has sunk into it. Wood in that spot stays damp and gives termites a ready path up to the frame. If you have these under your home, it is a good reason to talk to a crew about swapping them for rated plastic, composite or steel shims on solid pier pads. Our post on [mobile home leveling shims](/blog/mobile-home-leveling-shims/) explains what holds up and what does not.
- **Skirting framing.** Wood furring strips, 2x4 frames and wood lattice that run into the dirt hold water and lead termites to the home's trim and floor edge. HUD's installation standards require wood skirting within 6 inches of the ground to be pressure-treated for ground contact or naturally resistant to decay and termite infestations ([24 CFR 3285.504(c)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-24/section-3285.504)).
- **Wood blocking on the soil.** Untreated wood stacked on bare dirt as a support or spacer stays damp and is easy for termites to reach.
- **Stored wood.** Firewood, scrap lumber and cardboard stored under the home are a free meal. Pull it out.

## Damaged Wood: What It Looks and Feels Like

Termites eat wood from the inside, so damaged wood can look fine on the surface. To check, tap it with a screwdriver handle.

- Solid wood gives a sharp knock.
- Damaged wood sounds hollow or papery.
- A screwdriver tip pushes in easily.
- You may see wood that looks layered, with thin shells of surface wood over empty galleries.

Those signs can also mean plain rot from moisture, which is common in mobile homes and not caused by termites at all. We cannot tell you which it is from a look and a poke, and a pest control company can. Either way, it is wood damage that needs repair. ## Signs You Can See From Inside the Home

You do not have to crawl under the house to catch a problem. Inside, watch for:

- Swarmers, which are winged insects, or piles of discarded wings near windows, doors and light fixtures.
- Soft or spongy floor spots, especially near bathrooms, kitchens and exterior doors.
- Paint or paneling that bubbles, sags or sounds hollow when you tap it.
- Doors and windows that start sticking for no clear reason. This also happens when a home settles, so it is not proof of anything, but the two can overlap.
- Tiny pellet-like droppings below wood, which come from drywood termites rather than the ground-dwelling kind.

## What to Do If You Find Signs

1. **Do not spray store-bought products and call it done.** Surface sprays can scatter a colony and make treatment harder.
2. **Call a licensed pest control company** and ask for a termite inspection. Tell them it is a mobile home and that you have a crawlspace.
3. **Leave the evidence in place.** Photograph the mud tubes and damaged wood where they are.
4. **Ask about a written report and treatment plan** before agreeing to anything. If you are buying the home, ask whether a wood-destroying organism report is needed for your loan.
5. **Fix the moisture afterward.** Treatment kills what is there. It does not fix a wet crawlspace, and a wet crawlspace is what brought them.

## Keep Moisture Down Under the Home

You cannot make a home termite-proof, but you can make it less appealing. The same conditions that breed mold are the ones termites like, so most of it comes down to keeping the space dry:

- **Ground vapor barrier.** A torn, missing or pushed-aside barrier lets ground moisture rise into the crawlspace. HUD's standards call for a minimum of six mil polyethylene or equivalent covering the ground under a skirted home ([24 CFR 3285.204](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-24/section-3285.204)). Our [vapor barrier service in Ocala](/vapor-barrier-ocala/) replaces and repairs it.
- **Drainage.** Gutters, downspouts and grading should send rain away from the home, not into the crawlspace. If you see pooled water, read our post on [water under a mobile home](/blog/water-under-mobile-home/).
- **Clear vents.** Blocked skirting vents trap humidity.
- **No wood on the dirt.** Replace wood shims and wood skirting framing that touch soil.
- **Leaks.** A dripping drain or supply line feeds termites as well as mold. We do not do plumbing, so call a plumber for those.

## Do Not Crawl Under a Jacked Home

If you do go under to look, never get under a home that is supported only by a jack. Jacks slip. Look from the crawlspace edge with a flashlight, or have a crew do it. Watch for animals, wasps and standing water too, and do not go in at all if you smell gas or see exposed wiring.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are the first signs of termites under a mobile home?

Mud tubes on piers, blocks or the underside of the floor are the most common first sign. Other early signs are wood that sounds hollow when tapped, swarmer wings near windows and doors, and soft floor spots. If you see any of these, call a licensed pest control company for an inspection.

### Do you treat termites?

No. We do not do pest control. We level homes, install tie-downs, repair vapor barriers and install and repair skirting. If we notice mud tubes or damaged wood while we are under a home, we will point it out and suggest you call a licensed pest control company.

### Can termites cause a mobile home to sag or sink?

Termites can weaken floor joists, rim framing and any wood used as blocking, which can cause floors to sag. A home that is sinking because piers are settling into sandy soil is a separate problem. A level check and an underside look can tell the two apart.

### Do wood shims attract termites?

Wood shims that sit on bare soil or in damp spots can give termites a path to the frame. Shims that are dry and well above the ground are less of a risk, but a releveling is a good time to swap old wood shims for rated plastic, composite or steel ones.

### Will a vapor barrier stop termites?

No. A ground vapor barrier controls moisture rising from the soil. It does not stop termites, and a barrier that is torn or pushed aside can hide termite tubes. Keeping the crawlspace dry makes it a less attractive spot, but treatment is the pest control company's job.
