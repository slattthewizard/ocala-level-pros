# Mobile Home Underpinning (Skirting): What It Is and How to Pick One

Mobile home underpinning is the wall that closes in the space between the bottom of the home and the ground. In most of the country, and in Marion County, people use the word interchangeably with skirting. The material you pick matters less than whether the wall has vents, an access opening, and room for the home to move.

This guide explains where the word comes from, what any underpinning has to do, the common options at a glance, and the mistakes that cost homeowners money. If you want the full comparison of materials, we wrote that separately and link it below.

## Underpinning vs Skirting: Same Thing, Mostly

Search for underpinning and you will find two meanings. In the building trades, underpinning can mean reinforcing or deepening a foundation under an existing structure. That is concrete and excavation work, and it has nothing to do with a panel wall around a mobile home.

For a manufactured home, underpinning almost always means the perimeter enclosure. Park rules, insurance forms, and older listings use it. HUD's installation rules use the word skirting instead, and define it as a weather-resistant material used to enclose the perimeter, under the living area of the home, from the bottom of the home to grade. The word underpinning does not appear in those rules, so when a code or inspector talks about it, they mean skirting.

A few regions use underpinning for a heavier masonry wall and skirting for vinyl or metal panels. That split is not universal. If a contractor quotes you underpinning, ask what it is made of and whether it sits on a footing. Those two answers tell you what you are actually buying.

## What Underpinning Is For

Underpinning is a weather and pest barrier. It is not a foundation, and it does not hold the home up or hold it down. The home sits on its piers and is held to the ground by its tie-downs. Underpinning only closes the gap.

In Marion County that job is bigger than it sounds:

- It keeps rain splash and blown debris off the piers, pads, and ground.
- It keeps raccoons, armadillos, rats, and stray cats out from under the floor.
- It shades the belly and ductwork from direct sun.
- It makes the home look finished, which matters in a park with appearance rules and at a sale.

It also changes what happens to moisture. Once the space is enclosed, humid Florida air can sit under the home and condense on cool surfaces. That is why the next section matters more than the material.

## What Any Underpinning Must Do

Federal installation rules set a few requirements that apply no matter what the wall is made of. These are the ones a homeowner can check from the yard.

**Ventilation.** A crawlspace with skirting must have ventilation openings. The minimum net area is one square foot for every 150 square feet of the home's floor area, and that can drop to one square foot for every 1,500 square feet when a vapor retarder covers the ground under the entire home. Openings go as high as practicable and on at least two opposite sides, and each one is covered with a perforated, corrosion-resistant, rodent-proof covering ([24 CFR 3285.505](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-24/section-3285.505)). Our post on [mobile home skirting vents](/blog/mobile-home-skirting-vents/) works through the math.

**An access opening.** The same section calls for an access opening at least 18 inches wide and 24 inches high, and at least three square feet in area, located so any utility connections under the home can be reached. A wall with no access forces a plumber or a leveling crew to tear something out.

**Weather-resistant material, attached the right way.** Skirting must be weather-resistant or protected against weather deterioration. It must not be attached in a way that traps water between the siding and trim, or forces water up into the wall cavities. It also must not impede the expansion and contraction of the home's exterior covering. Wood within 6 inches of the ground must be pressure-treated or naturally resistant to decay and termites ([24 CFR 3285.504](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-24/section-3285.504)).

**Ground moisture control.** If the space is enclosed, a vapor retarder must cover the ground under the home, unless it is in an arid region with dry soil. Marion County is not arid. Our [vapor barrier service](/vapor-barrier-ocala/) covers that side.

## It Must Not Carry the Home

This is the one people get wrong. Underpinning should never be load-bearing. If the home settles onto the wall, the wall can crack, buckle, or crush, and the home is no better supported.

Marion County soil is sandy. Heavy summer rain moves it, and piers can sink unevenly over the years. Leave room for that movement. Vinyl and foam panels have some give. A rigid masonry wall does not, so the home needs to be level and stable before one goes up, and the wall should stand on its own footing rather than under the home's frame.

If your underpinning is bowed, pinched at the top, or pulled out of its track along one side, read that as a warning about the home, not just the panels. Our post on [mobile home skirting buckling](/blog/mobile-home-skirting-buckling/) walks through the signs.

| Option | What it is | What to know |
|---|---|---|
| Vinyl panels | Snap-in panels in a top and bottom track | Cheapest, easy to repair, can go brittle in Florida sun |

## Before You Pay for Underpinning

Run through this list before you sign anything:

1. **Look at the level.** Doors that stick, a floor that slopes, or gaps at the corners mean the home is moving. Fix that first.
2. **Look at the ground.** Bare dirt under the home needs a vapor barrier before it gets closed in.
3. **Count the vents and find the access.** Ask where they go and how big they are.
4. **Check the anchors and piers.** New underpinning will hide rusted straps and sinking piers, not fix them. Photograph them first.
5. **Check park or HOA rules.** Some parks dictate color or material.
6. **Get it in writing.** Material, height, vents, access, and what happens to the old skirting.

Never crawl under a home that is supported only by a jack, and do not try to jack a home yourself to fit a wall.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is mobile home underpinning the same as skirting?

In most places, yes. Underpinning is the older or regional word for the wall that closes in the space between the home and the ground. HUD's installation rules call it skirting. A few regions use underpinning for a heavier masonry wall, so ask any contractor what material they mean.

### Does mobile home underpinning hold the home up?

No. The home rests on its piers and is held down by its tie-downs. Underpinning is a weather and pest barrier. It should never carry the home's weight, and if the home settles onto it, the wall can crack or buckle.

### Does underpinning need vents and an access opening?

Yes. Federal installation rules call for ventilation openings sized by floor area, placed on at least two opposite sides, and an access opening at least 18 inches wide, 24 inches high, and three square feet in area. The material does not change that.

### Should I level the home before installing underpinning?

Yes. Underpinning is cut to the home's current height. If the home settles afterward, panels buckle and gaps open, and a rigid wall can crack. A leveling inspection is $150-$250, and it costs far less than redoing a skirting job.
