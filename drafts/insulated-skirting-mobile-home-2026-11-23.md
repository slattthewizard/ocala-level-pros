# Insulated Skirting for Mobile Homes: Does It Help in Florida?

Insulated skirting for mobile homes helps a little, and mostly in the direction people do not expect. It slows the temperature swing in the space under the home, which takes the edge off a cold January night more than it cuts a July power bill. It does not replace the insulation in the floor, and it cannot seal the crawlspace, because the vents have to stay.

That is the honest short answer. Below is what insulated skirting is, what it does in Marion County weather, where it goes wrong, and when it is worth the extra money. We install rigid-foam skirting, so we have a stake in this, and we would still rather you buy it for the right reasons.

## What Insulated Skirting Actually Is

The label covers two different products, and sales pages mix them up.

**Rigid-foam skirting panels.** A foam board, usually polystyrene or similar, shaped and coated with a hard textured face, often in a stone or brick pattern. The foam is the core of the panel. This is the insulated, rigid-foam skirting we install. Our post on [faux stone skirting for mobile homes](/blog/faux-stone-skirting-mobile-home/) covers the look, the weight, and how the panels fit.

**Foam board behind ordinary skirting.** A sheet of rigid foam cut to fit against the inside face of vinyl or other skirting, or fastened to the wall of the crawlspace. The skirting is still vinyl. The foam is a second layer behind it.

Some "insulated vinyl" skirting has a thin foam backing bonded to the panel. Treat those like any other product and ask for the manufacturer's written spec on that exact panel before you believe a number.

## Where the Real Insulation Lives

Look at how a mobile home is built. The insulation that matters is in the floor, in the cavity between the floor above and the belly board below, plus whatever is in the walls and roof. The belly board, also called the bottom board or underbelly, holds that insulation up and keeps rodents and moisture out of it.

When the belly and floor insulation are intact, your living space is already separated from the air under the home. The skirting is a second wall around a space the floor is already insulated against.

When the belly is torn, sagging, or missing, no skirting fixes that. Cold and humid air moves right into the floor cavity. Look at the belly first. Our guide to [belly board repair](/blog/mobile-home-belly-board-repair/) shows what a damaged one looks like, and the [underside inspection checklist](/blog/mobile-home-underside-inspection-checklist/) walks through everything else under there.

## Does Insulated Skirting Help in Florida?

It depends on the season, and the answer differs for heating and cooling.

**Winter.** Marion County gets cold snaps. Skirting that holds the under-home air a few degrees warmer than the outside helps the floor feel less cold and takes some strain off pipes in the belly on a freezing night. This is where insulated panels earn their keep, though only to a modest degree. A frozen pipe is still possible if the home is poorly sealed or the skirting is damaged.

**Summer.** Most of the Florida energy bill is cooling. Heat reaches a mobile home mainly through the roof and walls and through sunlit surfaces, not up from the shaded ground. Insulated skirting shades the space under the home and slows heat gain from the ground a little, but we would not expect a big change in a July bill from skirting alone. Ducts in the belly that are leaky, wet, or crushed will waste far more than the skirting can save.

We do not promise a dollar saving and we do not quote an R-value for your home. The result depends on your floor insulation, belly board, duct condition, and how tight the underside is. If a seller gives you a savings figure, ask for the written source.

## It Cannot Be Sealed Tight

This is where people get into trouble. Foam panels are solid, and they fit tightly, so it is tempting to close every gap. Do not.

Federal installation rules require ventilation openings for a crawlspace with skirting. The minimum net area is one square foot for every 150 square feet of floor area, reducible to one square foot for every 1,500 square feet when a vapor retarder covers the ground under the whole home. Openings go as high as practicable on at least two opposite sides, covered with a rodent-proof screen. An access opening of at least 18 inches by 24 inches and three square feet is also required ([24 CFR 3285.505](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-24/section-3285.505)). Insulation does not change any of that.

Florida humidity is the reason it matters. Warm, wet air gets into the space, and if it cannot leave, it condenses on cool surfaces, such as ducts, pipes, and the underside of the floor. Our post on [mobile home skirting vents](/blog/mobile-home-skirting-vents/) explains how many vents your home needs.

## Moisture: The Real Risk

Insulated skirting makes the crawlspace more stable, and that cuts both ways. A dry, ventilated space stays dry longer. A wet one stays wet longer, because the panels slow the drying.

Three things control it:

1. **A ground vapor barrier.** If the space is enclosed with skirting, a vapor retarder must cover the ground under the home, unless the home is in an arid region with dry soil ([24 CFR 3285.204](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-24/section-3285.204)). Marion County is not arid. Our [vapor barrier service](/vapor-barrier-ocala/) covers repairs and replacement.
2. **Drainage.** Regrade so water runs away from the home. Standing water under insulated skirting grows mold in a hurry.
3. **A clean seal between panel and ground.** Soil washed against or over the bottom edge holds water against the panel.

If you already smell mildew under the home or see water after rain, fix that before you add any skirting. Our post on [water under a mobile home](/blog/water-under-mobile-home/) covers what to do.

## Foam Board Behind Vinyl: Does It Work?

It can, with limits. Foam board fitted behind vinyl skirting adds some insulation without changing the look. Some things to know before you try it, whether you do it or hire it out:

- The vents and the access panel still have to stay open. Cut the foam around them, do not cover them.
- Foam needs to stay out of the ground track and off wet soil.
- Termites and other insects can tunnel through foam board, and a solid layer against the wall can hide the tunnels. Keep the bottom edge off the soil and keep up your pest inspections.
- Do not wedge it so tightly that it pushes the vinyl out. Vinyl needs room to expand in the heat.
- Do not glue or screw foam to the home's siding or rim. Skirting must not be attached in a way that traps water between the siding and trim, or that blocks the siding's expansion and contraction ([24 CFR 3285.504](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-24/section-3285.504)).

Work from outside where you can. Never get under a home supported only by a jack.

## When Insulated Skirting Is Worth It

It makes sense when:

- The belly board and floor insulation are intact and the ducts are in decent shape.
- The ground is dry and has, or will get, a proper vapor barrier.
- You want a stiffer, better-looking wall than thin vinyl, and the cold snaps bother you.
- The home is level, so the panels will not get crushed or pulled apart.

It does not make sense when:

- The home is out of level. Check the signs in our post on [when a mobile home needs leveling](/blog/signs-mobile-home-needs-leveling/).
- The belly is damaged or ducts are leaking. Fix those first.
- Water stays under the home after rain.
- The goal is a big power-bill drop. Skirting is rarely the biggest lever.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does insulated skirting help in Florida?

A little. It slows the temperature swing under the home, which helps most on cold winter nights. It does not do much about summer cooling bills, because most of that heat comes through the roof and walls. The floor insulation, belly board, and ducts matter more than the skirting.

### Can insulated skirting seal the crawlspace?

No. Federal installation rules require ventilation openings sized by floor area and an access opening, no matter what the skirting is made of. Sealing the space tight traps humid Florida air and invites mold and condensation.

### Can I put foam board behind my vinyl skirting?

You can, but leave the vents and the access panel open, keep the foam out of the ground track and off wet soil, and leave vinyl room to expand in the heat. Foam board can hide termite tunnels, so keep up your pest inspections.

### Is rigid-foam skirting worth the extra cost over vinyl?

It is worth it if you want a stiffer, better-looking wall and the home is level with a dry underside. It costs more, roughly $2,200-$3,500 for us against roughly $1,200-$2,000 for vinyl on a single-wide, so do not buy it expecting a big power-bill drop.

### Do I need a vapor barrier under insulated skirting?

Yes, in Florida. Federal installation rules require a vapor retarder over the ground under a home enclosed with skirting, unless the home is in an arid region with dry soil. Marion County is not arid.
