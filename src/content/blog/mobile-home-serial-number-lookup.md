A mobile home serial number is in three places: on the data plate inside the home, stamped into the steel frame at the hitch end, and on the title. To look a home up in Florida, start with that number, then take it to the county tax collector's tag office and the county property appraiser. The tax collector handles the title and the yearly decal. The property appraiser's record shows how the home is classed for tax purposes.

There is no single free website that returns a full history from a serial number. A lookup in Florida is really a matching job: the number on the home, the number on the paperwork and the number on the labels all have to agree. Below: where to find each number, what a mismatch means, and what to check on the home before money changes hands.

## Serial Number or VIN: Same Thing?

People use the two words interchangeably, and for most homes the serial number the factory stamped is the number that ends up on the title as the VIN. That is the usual case, not a guarantee, since some states and older titles handle it differently. Compare the digits character by character and treat any difference as a question to answer before you buy.

The serial number is assigned at the factory. Under the federal construction standard it has to identify the manufacturer and the state where the home was built ([24 CFR § 3280.6](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-24/section-3280.6)). It is not a code you can decode yourself without the manufacturer's own key, so do not trust a website that claims to read the age or floor plan from the digits alone.

## Where to Find the Serial Number on a Florida Mobile Home

You have three places to look, and you want all three.

**1. The data plate inside the home.** Federal rules require a data plate on every dwelling unit, fixed near the main electrical panel or in another readily accessible spot. It lists the serial number, the model, the factory name and address, and the date of manufacture ([24 CFR § 3280.5](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-24/section-3280.5)). Look inside a kitchen cabinet, a bedroom closet or the utility area first. We cover how to read it line by line in [our guide to the HUD data plate](/blog/mobile-home-hud-data-plate/).

**2. The steel frame.** Federal rules require the serial number to be stamped into the foremost crossmember of the frame, not into the hitch or drawbar ([24 CFR § 3280.6](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-24/section-3280.6)). That is the front steel crossbeam at the tongue end. Check from the outside: the stamp is on the underside structure, and it is easier to find when the belly board or skirting is open at that end. Use a flashlight and a wire brush, because paint, rust and road grime hide a stamp that is only a few characters tall.

**3. The title or registration.** The title and the Florida registration certificate both carry the number. The Marion County Tax Collector's page on mobile homes says that if a registration certificate is lost, an owner can use the most current decal number or the VIN to start the replacement ([mariontax.com](https://mariontax.com/mobile-homes)).

### Double-wides

A double-wide is built and shipped as two sections, and the Marion County Tax Collector treats each section as a separate unit. The data plate is one per home, but the HUD certification label is one per transportable section, so a double-wide carries two. Many double-wides use the same base serial number with a letter on the end, often A for one section and B for the other, but the exact convention depends on the manufacturer. Read what is actually stamped and printed on your paperwork rather than assuming the pattern.

The tax collector's page says double-wide and triple-wide homes count as two and three separate units, and each unit must have a decal. So a double-wide should show two decals, not one.

## The Three Numbers That Have to Match

When a home sells, three things get compared:

- the serial number stamped on the frame and printed on the data plate,
- the number on the title and registration,
- the HUD certification label numbers (the label is separate from the serial number; each section has its own, and the data plate lists them).

If the frame stamp, the data plate and the title all agree, the home is who the paperwork says it is. If they do not, stop. A transposed digit on an old title is a question for the tax collector's tag office, and it is far better to sort it out before closing than after.

A missing label is a separate problem. If a section has lost its HUD label, a lender or the park may ask for proof the home was built to the federal standard. We wrote about that in [how a HUD label verification letter works](/blog/hud-label-verification-letter/). It can slow a sale or a loan, so raise it early.

If the frame stamp is missing or painted over, that is a red flag, but you can still work with it. Note it, photograph the data plate and the label, and ask the seller how the title was issued.

## How to Look a Home Up in Marion County

Florida does not give you one national-style history report for a manufactured home. You assemble it from a few offices. Here is the order we would use in Marion County.

**Step 1: Get the number and the paperwork from the seller.** Ask for a photo of the title front and back, the current registration, and the last decal. Then verify the number on the home yourself.

**Step 2: Ask the tax collector's tag office.** The Marion County Tax Collector issues the numbered mobile home decal, and its page says the tag office processes the transfer into the new owner's name before registration. The documents the office lists for registering include the current or last Florida registration certificate, the title if there is no registration certificate, or a title signed off by the previous owner plus a bill of sale. If the home is financed, the page says to get a copy from the lien holder. Call or visit before you pay anything. Fees are listed on [the tax collector's mobile home page](https://mariontax.com/mobile-homes).

**Step 3: Check the decal.** Florida mobile home registrations renew every year by December 31, and the tax collector's page says the decal must sit in a window clearly visible from the road in front of the home. A home with an expired decal or none at all is a home whose paperwork needs attention. Ask for proof of current registration.

**Step 4: Look at the property appraiser record.** The county property appraiser keeps a public record for the parcel. For a home on owned land, that record shows whether the home is treated as part of the real property. The Marion County Tax Collector says a home permanently affixed to the owner's land and declared as real property gets a permanent "RP" decal, and the owner first needs a DR-402 form from the Property Appraiser's office showing the land and home titles are in the same name. The same page says the RP decal is permanent and transfers to a new owner when the land and home are sold together ([RP decal page](https://mariontax.com/real-property-rp-decals)).

The two cases are handled differently at sale. A titled home on rented land in a park sells with a title. A home permanently affixed to owned land and declared real property is part of a land transaction. Do not guess which one you are looking at: the decal on the window and the appraiser record will tell you. For questions about titles, liens or how a record should read, the tax collector's tag office is the right place to ask. We do not do title work.

**Step 5: Ask about liens.** If the title shows a lien holder, the lien needs to be released or paid off at closing. Ask the tag office how a lien shows on the record.

## Finding the Age of Your Mobile Home

Most people hunting for the number want to know how old the home is. The fastest answer is the date of manufacture on the data plate. If the plate is gone, the title and registration records and the manufacturer's records are the next place to look.

Age matters in Florida for practical reasons. Homes built before 1976 were made before the federal HUD standard, and insurers, lenders and parks treat them differently. We cover what that means in [pre-1976 mobile homes in Florida](/blog/pre-1976-mobile-homes-florida/). Wind-load rules on the plate also tell you which wind zone the home was built for, and Marion County is HUD Wind Zone II.

## What the Serial Number Cannot Tell You

A clean paperwork match says nothing about the condition of the home. A serial number will not show you:

- whether the piers have settled,
- whether the tie-downs are rusted or missing,
- whether there is water under the home,
- whether the belly board is torn,
- whether the skirting is hiding any of it.

That is why the paperwork check is step one, not the whole inspection. Walk the home with our [used mobile home inspection checklist](/blog/used-mobile-home-inspection-checklist/) in hand. Doors that stick, cracks over windows and bouncy spots in the floor often point to a home that has moved off level.

An underside look is the part most buyers skip. Anchors and straps, pier condition, the vapor barrier and the belly wrap are all under there. ## Quick Checklist Before You Close

- Find the serial number in all three places: data plate, frame, title.
- Confirm they match, character for character.
- Count the HUD labels against the number of sections.
- Confirm the decal is current and displayed, or that an RP decal applies.
- Check the property appraiser record to see whether the home is classed as real property.
- Ask the tag office about liens and how the transfer works.
- Have the underside inspected before you sign.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Where is the serial number on a mobile home?

Look in three places: the data plate inside the home (often near the electrical panel or in a cabinet or closet), the steel frame, where federal rules require the number to be stamped into the foremost crossmember at the tongue end, and the title. A double-wide has a data plate for the whole home and each section has its own frame, so check the front crossmember of both.

### Is the VIN the same as the serial number?

Usually the VIN on a Florida title matches the factory serial number, but compare them yourself and do not assume. If the title and the home show different numbers, ask the Marion County Tax Collector's tag office how the title was issued before you buy.

### Can I look up a mobile home by serial number for free?

There is no single free database that returns a full history. You can confirm the title and decal status with the county tax collector, check the parcel record with the county property appraiser, and read the date of manufacture off the data plate. Be cautious about paid sites that promise a full report from the digits alone.

### What if the data plate or HUD label is missing?

Write down what is missing and photograph the frame stamp and any labels that are there. Ask the seller how the home was titled, and see our article on the [HUD label verification letter](/blog/hud-label-verification-letter/) for how a missing label is handled. A missing plate or label can slow a loan or a move, so raise it early.
