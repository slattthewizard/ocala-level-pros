Here's a stat that surprises most homeowners we meet: about 7 out of 10 skirting problems we get called out for in Marion County have nothing to do with the skirting itself. The panels bowed, buckled, or pulled away from the home because the home moved, not the vinyl.

That matters because mobile home skirting buckling is one of the earliest and most visible warning signs of settling. Your skirting is basically a giant level wrapped around your house. When piers sink even half an inch, the skirting is often the first thing to show it. Replace the panels without fixing the settling underneath, and you'll be replacing them again in a year or two.

In this guide we'll walk through why skirting buckles, how to tell whether it's a skirting problem or a foundation problem, what each fix actually costs here in Ocala and Marion County, and when it's safe to DIY versus when you need a pro under the home.

## Why Mobile Home Skirting Buckles When the Ground Shifts

Skirting is installed to fit the gap between the bottom of your home and the ground. That gap is supposed to stay put. In Central Florida, it often doesn't.

Your mobile home sits on piers, usually concrete blocks resting on soil. Florida's sandy soil compacts, washes, and shifts, especially after our summer rain pattern dumps water around the perimeter of the home month after month. When a pier settles, that section of the home drops with it. The gap the skirting was cut for gets shorter, and the panels have nowhere to go but out or sideways. That's the buckle.

The reverse happens too. If one side of the home settles, the opposite side can effectively lift relative to the skirting. Now the panels hang loose, pull out of the top track, or leave a visible gap you can see daylight through. Homeowners usually describe it as "skirting pulling away from the mobile home," and it's the same root cause from the other direction.

A few other clues tell you settling is the driver:

- The buckling is concentrated on one side or one corner, not spread evenly around the home
- Panels that were re-seated pop back out within weeks
- You've also noticed doors sticking, floors sloping, or new drywall cracks inside
- The problem showed up or got worse after a heavy wet season

If any of those sound familiar, check out our post on the [signs your mobile home needs leveling](/blog/signs-mobile-home-needs-leveling/). Skirting is usually just the first domino.

One Florida-specific note: heat can warp vinyl, and a mower can crack panels. Those are genuine skirting-only problems, but heat warping shows up as shallow ripples across sunny walls, and impact damage is obvious. A deep bow or a separating top track points at movement.

## How to Tell If It's the Skirting or the Home That Moved

You can do a rough diagnosis yourself in about 20 minutes, and none of it requires crawling under the home.

**Step 1: Walk the perimeter.** Note exactly where the skirting is buckled, bowed, or gapped. Sketch it on paper if that helps. Settling problems cluster. Random, scattered warping across all four sides usually points to sun damage or cheap panels instead.

**Step 2: Check the top track.** The skirting's top rail attaches to the home. If the rail is still straight but the panels below it bow, the ground or the panels moved. If the rail itself dips or angles, the home's rim is no longer level, and that's a leveling issue.

**Step 3: Look at the gap.** Measure from the bottom of the siding to the ground at a few points around the home. On a level home, that measurement changes gradually with the grade. A sudden half-inch to one-inch difference over a short run is a red flag for a settled pier right behind that spot.

**Step 4: Go inside.** Check the rooms directly above the worst skirting section. Sticking doors, cracked corners over door frames, and that subtle "walking downhill" feeling all back up the settling theory.

**Step 5: Set a marble on the floor.** If it rolls consistently toward the wall with the buckled skirting, you have your answer.

## What Buckled Skirting Costs to Ignore in Marion County

Buckled or gapped skirting isn't just cosmetic. Once panels separate, you've opened the door to three expensive follow-on problems.

**Moisture.** Gaps let wind-driven rain under the home, where it soaks the ground around your piers and speeds up the very settling that caused the gap. It also puts your [vapor barrier](/vapor-barrier-ocala/) and belly wrap at risk. Wet insulation and mold remediation under a mobile home routinely run into four figures.

**Animals.** An opening the width of your fist is an engraved invitation for raccoons, opossums, and stray cats. They tear belly board, nest in insulation, and chew what they find. Cleanup and repair after an animal moves in typically costs far more than the skirting fix would have.

**Inspection and insurance headaches.** If you sell, or your insurer orders a 4-point inspection, failing skirting invites a closer look, and inspectors who find gaps almost always go looking for the settling behind them.

## Real Fixes: From Releveling to Skirting Repair

When settling is the cause, the fix happens in a specific order. Doing it backwards wastes money.

**First, the home gets releveled.** A crew checks the level along the I-beams with a water level or laser, then uses jacks to bring low spots back to spec, adjusting or shimming piers as they go. Most single wides take two to four hours; double wides usually take four to eight. Typical cost in our service area runs $450 to $900 for a standard relevel, more if piers need replacing. The HUD installation standards that govern manufactured home support and anchoring are worth a skim if you want the technical background; you can find them on [HUD's manufactured housing program pages](https://www.hud.gov/program_offices/housing/rmra/mhs).

**Second, the cause gets addressed where possible.** If water pooling against one side washed out the soil, we'll flag drainage fixes like gutter downspout extensions or regrading. If the pier pads were undersized for the soil, that gets corrected during the relevel. Our post on [mobile home settling after rain](/blog/mobile-home-settling-after-rain/) explains why Florida's wet season does so much of this damage.

**Third, the skirting gets refit.** Once the home is back at its proper height, most vinyl skirting can be re-seated, re-cut, or re-tracked rather than replaced. Panels that are sun-brittle, cracked, or chewed get swapped. If your skirting is at the end of its life anyway, this is the right moment to upgrade, and our [mobile home skirting service in Ocala](/mobile-home-skirting-ocala/) can handle the whole package in one visit.

**Fourth, the underside gets a once-over.** While the crew is down there, it costs nothing extra to eyeball the vapor barrier, belly board, and tie-downs. Long-running settling often stresses anchor straps, and FEMA's building science guidance treats the support and anchoring system as one unit. Their resources at [fema.gov](https://www.fema.gov/emergency-managers/risk-management/building-science) are a solid plain-language reference.

## Can You Fix Buckled Skirting Yourself?

Some of this is honest DIY territory. Some of it isn't. Here's where we'd draw the line.

**Reasonable DIY:**

- Re-seating a panel that popped out of its track after a storm
- Replacing a single cracked panel with a matching one from the hardware store
- Trimming vegetation and moving sprinkler heads that spray the skirting
- Extending downspouts and improving drainage away from the home
- Measuring gaps and photographing problem areas to share with a contractor

**Stop and call a professional when:**

- The buckling comes back after you re-seat panels
- You can see or measure a height difference along the rim of the home
- Interior symptoms show up: sticking doors, sloped floors, wall cracks
- Any part of the fix involves jacking the home or adjusting piers

We want to be direct about that last one. Releveling means lifting a structure that weighs 15,000 to 40,000 pounds on hydraulic jacks. Blocks can shift, jacks can kick out, and being pinned under a home is not a survivable mistake. Professionals carry the right jacks, cribbing, and insurance for a reason. Skirting panels, yes. Jacks, no.

There's also a paperwork reason to use a pro. In Florida, work on the support and anchoring system ties into your tie-down certification, which insurers ask about during 4-point inspections. A documented relevel from a licensed company is an asset when you sell or renew a policy. A DIY jack job is a question mark at best.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does buckled skirting always mean my mobile home is settling?

No, but it's the most common cause we find in Marion County. Heat warping, impact damage from mowers, and poorly cut panels can all buckle skirting on their own. The tell is the pattern: settling causes buckling concentrated in one area, often paired with sticking doors or sloped floors inside, while heat and installation problems tend to be scattered and shallow.

### How much does it cost to fix skirting that's pulling away from a mobile home?

If the panels just need re-seating, it might cost nothing but an afternoon. If the home has settled, plan on $450 to $900 for a standard relevel in the Ocala area, plus $150 to $400 to refit or partially replace skirting. Full skirting replacement runs $1,400 to $3,200 for most homes, which is why it pays to rule out settling before buying new panels.

### Can I just push the skirting panels back into the track?

You can, and for storm-related pop-outs that's often all it takes. But if the same panels come loose again within a few weeks or months, the gap they're spanning has changed size, and that means the home moved. At that point, re-seating panels is treating the symptom, and it's time for a leveling check.

### How fast should I deal with skirting gaps?

Sooner than feels urgent, honestly. Open skirting lets rain soak the soil around your piers, which accelerates settling, and it gives animals a way under the home where they can damage insulation and the belly board. Most homeowners who wait a year end up paying two to three times more than the original fix would have cost.
