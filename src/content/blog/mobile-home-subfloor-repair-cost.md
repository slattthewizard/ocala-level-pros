Here's the short version of mobile home subfloor repair cost in Marion County: a single patch typically runs $300 to $700, a room usually lands between $900 and $2,500, and a full subfloor replacement in a single-wide or double-wide can run $6,000 to $12,000 or more. ## What Drives Mobile Home Subfloor Repair Cost in Central Florida

Every subfloor quote comes down to four things: how much area is damaged, what caused the damage, what's under the floor, and what's on top of it.

**Damaged area.** Subfloor material itself is cheap. A 4x8 sheet of 3/4 inch tongue-and-groove plywood runs $45 to $75 at Ocala-area suppliers. What you're really paying for is labor. Cutting out rot, sistering joists, and fitting new decking around plumbing takes time, and time is where the dollars add up.

**The cause.** This one matters more than most homeowners expect. If a one-time leak from a washing machine hose soaked a small area, you patch it and move on. But if moisture has been rising from the ground under your home for years, replacing the subfloor without fixing the moisture problem means you'll be paying for the same repair again in three to five years. That's why a good contractor crawls under the home before quoting anything.

**What's underneath.** Damaged joists, a torn belly board, or a failed vapor barrier all add to the bill. Joist sistering typically adds $100 to $300 per joist. If the belly wrap is hanging open and soaked, plan on another $500 to $1,500 depending on how much needs replacing. Our [vapor barrier service in Ocala](/vapor-barrier-ocala/) exists largely because of this exact chain reaction.

**What's on top.** Flooring has to come up before subfloor can come out. Vinyl plank is quick. Glued-down sheet vinyl, tile, or built-in cabinets slow everything down. If the damaged section runs under a bathroom vanity or kitchen island, expect the labor portion of the quote to climb.

One more Florida-specific factor: settling. When a mobile home sinks unevenly, floors flex more than they were designed to, seams open up, and water finds its way in faster. ## Patch Repair Cost: When a $300 Fix Is All You Need

A patch repair means cutting out the damaged section of subfloor back to the nearest joists, adding blocking for support, and screwing down new decking. Done right, a patch is a permanent repair, not a band-aid.

Typical patch pricing in the Ocala area:

- **Small soft spot (under 10 sq ft):** $300 to $500
- **Larger patch (10 to 30 sq ft):** $450 to $700
- **Patch plus one sistered joist:** $550 to $900
- **Bathroom patch around a toilet flange:** $500 to $1,000, since plumbing work and wax ring replacement usually come with it

Patching makes sense when the damage is isolated and the cause is fixed. A dropped aquarium, a one-time supply line leak, a window that got left open during a summer storm. If the surrounding wood is dry and solid, there's no reason to replace more than the bad section.

Here's what that looks like in practice. Denise, a homeowner in Belleview, called us about a spongy spot in front of her kitchen sink. The culprit was a slow drip at the dishwasher connection that had been fixed a year earlier, but the damage was already done. We cut out about 12 square feet of delaminated particleboard, added blocking, screwed down new plywood, and she was done for $460. The rest of her floor was solid, so replacing more would have been wasted money.

The key phrase there is "the rest of her floor was solid." A patch is only the right call when a pro has actually probed the surrounding subfloor and confirmed the rot stops where it looks like it stops. Rot travels along seams and under flooring where you can't see it. ## Full Subfloor Replacement Cost: What You're Really Paying For

Full replacement means stripping the finished flooring, removing all the old decking down to the joists, inspecting and repairing the frame, and laying new tongue-and-groove plywood throughout. It's a big job, and the pricing reflects that.

Typical full replacement pricing in Marion, Sumter, and Citrus counties:

- **Single room (bathroom or laundry):** $900 to $2,500
- **Single-wide, whole home:** $6,000 to $10,000
- **Double-wide, whole home:** $9,000 to $15,000
- **Add for joist repairs:** $100 to $300 per joist
- **Add for new finished flooring:** $2 to $6 per sq ft installed, depending on material

Why would anyone need the whole thing replaced? Two common reasons in our area. First, original particleboard. Many mobile homes built through the 1980s and into the 90s used particleboard subfloors, and particleboard turns to oatmeal when it gets wet. Once moisture has been in the belly of the home for years, the damage is rarely limited to one spot. Second, long-term ground moisture. A torn vapor barrier or standing water under the home slowly cooks the entire floor system from below. If you've seen puddles down there, read our post on [water under a mobile home](/blog/water-under-mobile-home/) because the floor is only one of the things at risk.

## Why Subfloors Fail in Marion County, and How to Stop Paying for It Twice

Subfloor damage is almost never the root problem. It's the symptom. Around here, the usual suspects are:

**Ground moisture.** Central Florida's water table sits high, and after a wet summer the soil under many homes stays damp for months. Without an intact vapor barrier, that moisture migrates straight up into the floor system. The [EPA's guidance on moisture and mold](https://www.epa.gov/mold) is blunt about it: you can't fix moisture damage without fixing the moisture.

**Plumbing leaks.** Supply lines, drain connections, and toilet flanges are behind the majority of bathroom subfloor failures. A leak that would be obvious on a slab home can drip into a mobile home's belly for months unseen.

**Torn belly board.** The belly wrap is your floor's last line of defense from below. Once it's torn, humidity, pests, and splashing rain all get access to bare wood. Our guide to [belly board repair](/blog/mobile-home-belly-board-repair/) explains why this cheap-looking material matters so much.

**Settling and flexing.** When piers sink or shims compress, the floor structure flexes beyond what it was built for. Seams in the decking work open, finished flooring cracks at the joints, and water from ordinary mopping and spills starts reaching the subfloor. HUD's construction standards for manufactured homes ([24 CFR Part 3280](https://www.hud.gov/program_offices/housing/rmra/mhs/mhshome)) assume the home sits on a properly supported, level foundation. When it doesn't, every system above it wears faster.

The practical takeaway: whatever repair path you choose, spend the extra hour finding the cause. A $460 patch that comes with a $200 plumbing fix is money well spent. A $460 patch over an active moisture problem is a down payment on the next repair.

## DIY or Call a Pro? Where to Draw the Line

Plenty of handy homeowners can patch a subfloor, and we'll never tell you otherwise. If the damage is small, away from plumbing, and you're comfortable cutting decking over joists, a DIY patch costs $60 to $150 in materials.

But there are hard lines where DIY stops making sense, and a few where it becomes genuinely unsafe:

- **Stop if the joists are soft.** Decking carries you to the joists. Joists carry the home. If a screwdriver sinks into a joist, the repair is structural and needs a pro.
- **Stop if you find mold across a wide area.** Small surface mold on wood you're removing anyway is manageable. Widespread growth in the belly or insulation is a health issue, not a carpentry issue.
- **Stop if the floor slopes as well as sags.** A sag is local damage. A slope usually means support problems underneath, and new plywood won't fix piers. Check the symptoms in our [signs your mobile home needs leveling](/blog/signs-mobile-home-needs-leveling/) guide before you buy lumber.
- **Stop if plumbing runs through the repair area.** One cracked drain fitting can turn a $400 patch into a much worse week.
- **Never crawl under a home that isn't properly supported.** If you have any doubt about the piers and blocking, stay out of the crawlspace. No repair is worth it.

There's also a paperwork reason to bring in a licensed contractor for bigger jobs. If you're selling, refinancing, or renewing insurance, a documented repair with a written invoice answers questions before they're asked. Florida insurers already scrutinize mobile homes during 4-point inspections, and "handyman patch, no receipt" is not the answer an underwriter wants to hear about floor damage.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does mobile home subfloor repair cost per square foot?

Figure $9 to $25 per square foot installed for typical repairs, with small patches costing more per foot because of setup time. Whole-home replacement usually averages out lower, around $6 to $12 per square foot, since crews work more efficiently across open areas. Joist repairs, plumbing work, and new finished flooring are extra.

### Can I just put new plywood over a rotten subfloor?

No, and any contractor who suggests it should worry you. Overlaying traps moisture, adds weight the rot-softened structure can't carry, and hides the damage while it spreads to joists. Rotten decking has to come out back to solid wood before anything new goes down.

### Does homeowners insurance cover subfloor repair in a mobile home?

Sometimes. Sudden events like a burst supply line are often covered, while long-term leaks, ground moisture, and general rot are almost always excluded as maintenance issues. Document the damage with photos the day you find it and call your agent before starting repairs, because how the claim is described matters.

### How long does a full subfloor replacement take?

Most single-wide replacements take 3 to 5 days, and double-wides run 5 to 7, assuming no major joist surprises. You can usually stay in the home while crews work room by room, though bathrooms may be out of service for a day. Weather matters less than you'd think since the work happens under a roof.
