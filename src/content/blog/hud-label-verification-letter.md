If the HUD tag on your mobile home is gone, painted over, or too corroded to read, the paper that replaces it is a HUD label verification letter from IBTS, the Institute for Building Technology and Safety. IBTS keeps manufacturer production records for HUD-code homes, and its own order site says the letter is a generally accepted substitute when the exterior labels are missing or unreadable. You will not get a new metal tag in the mail. You get a PDF.

To order it, you need the manufacturer's serial number or the full HUD label number with its three-letter prefix, plus the manufacturer's name. The rest of this guide covers where to find those, what the letter does and does not say, and who tends to ask for it.

## What the HUD Tag Is and Why Missing It Causes Trouble

The HUD tag is the certification label. Under [24 CFR § 3280.11](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-24/section-3280.11), it is a small etched aluminum plate, roughly 2 by 4 inches, riveted or screwed to the exterior of each transportable section. It sits at the taillight end, the end opposite the tow bar, about a foot up from the floor. A double-wide has two sections, so it should have two tags. A single-wide has one.

Tags go missing for boring reasons. Siding gets replaced, a porch or addition covers the end wall, someone paints the exterior, or the aluminum corrodes in Florida humidity. None of that changes the fact that the home was built to the HUD code. It just means you cannot prove it by pointing at the wall.

That proof matters when somebody else needs paperwork. We covered where each tag and plate lives, and what each one says, in our guide to the [HUD data plate on a mobile home](/blog/mobile-home-hud-data-plate/), so we will not repeat the location walkthrough here.

## Data Plate vs. HUD Label: Count Them Correctly

People mix these up, and so do some forms. The two items are counted differently.

The data plate is the paper document inside the home. [24 CFR § 3280.5](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-24/section-3280.5) requires one data plate per dwelling unit, so a double-wide has one. The HUD certification label is the aluminum tag outside, one per transportable section, so a double-wide has two.

That count shapes what you order. If a lender or insurer asks for "the HUD label numbers" on a double-wide, they want both section numbers. A letter that verifies only one of them leaves half the question open. When you place the order, enter every label number you know.

## Which IBTS Document Do You Need

IBTS sells two different products, and its order site spells out which is which.

Label Verification Letter: order this when the exterior HUD labels are missing or unreadable. IBTS lists what it contains: the label number, serial number, date of manufacture, manufacturer name and plant location, and the first destination, usually a dealer or retail center. It does not include wind zone, roof load or thermal zone information.

Data Plate / Performance Certificate: order this when the paper data plate inside the home is missing. It carries the serial number, date of manufacture, manufacturer, plant location, and the wind, roof load and thermal zones. It does not list label numbers. If IBTS cannot find a copy of the original data plate, it issues a substitute Performance Certificate, and the zones on it are based on where the home first shipped, usually a dealer, not where it sits now.

That last point matters if you live in Marion County. Our county sits in HUD Wind Zone II. A performance certificate reflects the zone for the home's first destination, so a home that first shipped to a dealer in another state can show a different zone than the one it lives in now. If an insurer is asking about wind rating, say so when you talk to them.

If both the tag and the plate are gone, you may need both documents. IBTS lets you order either one or both in the same request.

## Who Asks for This Letter

Nobody asks for it for fun. Insurance companies are the usual source: an underwriter wants proof the home is a HUD-code manufactured home, and when it was built, before writing or renewing a policy. Lenders and appraisers come next, since a loan on a manufactured home often depends on proving the home meets the HUD code. A buyer who sees no tag on the wall will ask how you know it is a HUD home. And a tax collector, property appraiser, or title office may want the serial number or label numbers when a home is retitled or converted to real property.

## What You Need Before You Order

IBTS says an order must include either the HUD label number or the manufacturer's serial number, along with the manufacturer name. Orders that arrive as placeholders without those details are cancelled at intake. Here is where the information tends to be hiding.

For the serial number, check these, roughly in this order:

1. The data plate inside the home, if you still have it.
2. The retired title, the certificate of origin, an appraisal, or an engineer report. IBTS lists these as places the serial number and label numbers show up.
3. An affidavit of affixture recorded with the county, if the home was converted to real property.
4. The front-facing crossbeam under the home. IBTS says the serial number is engraved there, and calls it the last resort if records are not available.

IBTS wants the number the manufacturer assigned. It does not work from title numbers, model numbers, or state-assigned VINs. Its form also warns that entering "unknown" in both the serial and label fields gets the order cancelled.

If you can see any trace of the tag, photograph it. IBTS asks for images of the metal HUD labels in some cases, and it may ask for a photo of the serial number on the crossbeam if your information conflicts with its records.

If you need a hand reading a serial number format, see our guide to [looking up a mobile home serial number](/blog/mobile-home-serial-number-lookup/).

Getting under the home to check the frame is the one step where we can help in person. A crossbeam stamped with a serial number is easier to read when someone has already cleared the insulation and animal debris away.

## How to Order From IBTS

The order form lives on IBTS's label verification site, [lvr2.ibts.org](https://lvr2.ibts.org/), which the main [IBTS manufactured housing page](https://www.ibts.org/manufactured-housing) points homeowners to. The steps, as the site presents them:

1. Choose the product: Label Verification Letter, Data Plate / Performance Certificate, or both.
2. Enter your contact information and the reason for the order, such as insurance or sale.
3. Enter the manufacturer's name and the serial number or HUD label number. Upload up to two supporting documents if you have them, and do not include Social Security numbers, birth dates, or driver's license numbers. IBTS says it never needs them.
4. Pick a processing speed and pay online.
5. Watch for the confirmation email. IBTS says it assigns an order number, and that you need it, plus the email on the order, to track the request.

Delivery is by email as a PDF. A mailed paper copy costs extra, and IBTS says the mail time is separate from processing time.

On fees, IBTS says a non-refundable research fee applies once a researcher is assigned to your order, and that it is included in the price. IBTS shows the full price for each product and speed at checkout, so check there before you pay. We are not going to quote a total that may have changed.

For speed, IBTS lists three tiers. Rush orders arrive the next business day. Super Rush orders arrive the same business day if placed before 1:00 p.m. Eastern on a business day. The regular and urgent day counts are shown on the order form, so we do not repeat them here. IBTS counts business days only, so a Saturday order is treated as placed on Monday.

IBTS also says it cannot search by property address, and that it cannot tell you whether a home was moved after it left the dealer. It works from the serial number or label number only.

## When IBTS Cannot Help

Two situations do not fit this route.

Homes built before June 15, 1976 are the first. The HUD code took effect that day. IBTS says it does not verify mobile homes built before it, and those homes never had HUD labels. If you own one, the answer is not a letter. Our guide to [pre-1976 mobile homes in Florida](/blog/pre-1976-mobile-homes-florida/) explains what insurers and lenders usually ask for instead.

Other factory-built structures are the second. IBTS says it does not verify modular homes, park models, RVs, or campers. They are built to different standards and have no HUD label.

If IBTS cannot find your home in its records, it may issue what it calls a specialized letter, which carries the same research fee. That is why double-checking the serial number before you submit pays off. A typo costs the fee and the wait.

## Why Insurers Ask for More Than the Letter

The HUD label letter proves what the home is. An insurer in Florida often wants to know how it is held down. A request that starts with the HUD label number can grow to include a tie-down certification letter and questions about whether the home is level and the piers are sound.

Those are separate documents, and the label verification does nothing for them. When an insurer sends you a list, work through it in this order:

1. Photograph the home and any surviving tag or plate today.
2. Order the IBTS letter if the tag or plate is missing. Start early, since processing is not instant.
3. Book the tie-down inspection while you wait, so both pieces are ready together.
4. Send the insurer everything in one email, with the order confirmation if the letter has not arrived.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I get a replacement HUD tag for my mobile home?

No. IBTS says original metal certification labels are not replaced if lost or destroyed, and that HUD does not reissue them. The label verification letter serves as the substitute documentation. Do not buy a lookalike tag from a stranger, since an altered or fake label can cause far more trouble than a missing one.

### Is a label verification letter the same as a data plate?

No. The letter verifies the HUD label numbers and the serial number, and it covers the exterior tags. The data plate, or the Performance Certificate that replaces it, carries the wind, roof load and thermal zones, and it does not list label numbers. If both are missing, you may need both documents.

### What if I cannot find the serial number or any label number?

IBTS says it cannot issue a verification without either the label number or the manufacturer's serial number and manufacturer name. Check the title, appraisal, and any engineer report first. As a last resort, look for the number engraved on the front crossbeam under the home. If you are not comfortable crawling under there, we can look during an underside inspection.

### Does a double-wide need one letter or two?

The letter covers the home, but the label numbers it verifies should include one for each section. A double-wide has one data plate and two HUD tags under the federal rules, so enter both label numbers when you know them. If you know only one, include the manufacturer's serial number too, since IBTS accepts either.
