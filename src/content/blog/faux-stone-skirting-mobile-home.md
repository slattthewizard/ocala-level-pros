Faux stone skirting is a lightweight panel, usually rigid foam or molded polymer with a stone pattern on the face, that wraps the bottom of a mobile home to look like a rock or ledgestone foundation. In Florida it costs more than vinyl, holds its shape better against sun and wind, and still needs vents and an access door like any other skirting. We price rigid-foam, insulated faux-stone skirting at about $2,200-$3,500 installed, against roughly $1,200-$2,000 for vinyl on a single-wide.

This guide covers what the panels are made of, how they behave in Marion County weather, what "insulated" does and does not buy you, and the details that decide whether an install lasts.

## What faux stone and rock skirting actually is

"Rock skirting" gets used for three different products, and sales pages rarely say which one you are looking at.

Foam-core panels are a rigid foam board, shaped or molded, then coated or faced with a hard, textured, stone-colored skin. One person can carry a panel and cut it with a saw. This is what most people mean by insulated skirting or rigid foam skirting.

Molded polymer panels are hollow or ribbed plastic pressed with a stone pattern. They are stiffer than vinyl and usually lack the foam core, so they are not insulated, but they carry the same look.

Real stone or block is masonry veneer over a block or concrete footing. That is a built foundation, not a skirting panel. It is concrete and masonry work, which we do not do, and it comes with different permitting.

The rest of this post is about the first two, mostly the foam-core kind. For how these compare with vinyl, metal and the other common materials, see [mobile home skirting options](/blog/mobile-home-skirting-options/).

## How stone-look skirting compares with vinyl

Vinyl is a thin, flexible panel. It is the cheapest way to close in the underside, and in Florida sun it goes brittle over the years, then wind and weed trimmers finish the job.

Stone-look foam panels differ in four ways.

Stiffness: a foam-core panel with a hard face does not flex in a gust the way a vinyl sheet does, and it resists a dent from a bumped mower. Look: the texture reads as masonry from the street, which matters to owners selling or trying to match a park's appearance rules. Weight: still light, so no heavy footing is needed under the panels. Price: higher, because the material costs more and the panels take longer to cut and fit around steps, corners and utility lines.

Neither material makes the home structurally stronger. Skirting is a cover, not a foundation. It will not hold up a sagging corner, and it does not replace leveling. If doors are sticking or floors are sloping, that is a [leveling problem](/mobile-home-leveling-ocala/) to fix before any skirting goes on.

## How it holds up to Florida sun, wind and wet sand

Sun: ultraviolet exposure is what ruins cheap vinyl. Stone-look panels have a pigmented, textured face that tends to hide chalking and fading better than a smooth vinyl sheet, but all of these products fade eventually, and a dark color on the south or west side gets the most sun. Ask the supplier how the face finish is rated for outdoor UV exposure and get the answer in writing, not as a brochure headline.

Wind: Marion County is in HUD Wind Zone II, and the home itself is anchored for that. Skirting is not a structural wall and is not designed to carry the home's wind load. What it has to do is stay attached in a storm so it does not become debris and leave the underside open to rain and animals. A stiffer panel flaps less than vinyl, but the attachment matters more than the panel: top and bottom fasteners, the right spacing, and corners that are closed properly.

Wet sand and rain: much of Marion County sits on sandy soil that drains fast in a dry week and floods after a summer downpour. Panel bottoms that sit in standing water or are buried in sand will stain, and foam can take on moisture at a cut edge. We set the bottom edge above grade or on a ground track, keep soil from piling against the panel, and make sure runoff from the roof edge and the AC drain goes away from the home, not into the crawlspace. If water is already sitting under your home, solve that before you cover it up.

## What "insulated" really buys you

Most listings for insulated skirting lead with energy savings, and that claim deserves some doubt.

An enclosed, insulated perimeter does slow the temperature swing in the crawlspace, so the floor can feel a little less cold on a freezing night, which Marion County does get. The wall also feels stiffer and quieter than a hollow vinyl sheet when you bump it.

We will not promise a dollar saving on your power bill or quote an R-value. We have no number for your home, and the result depends on the home's own floor insulation, the ducts, the condition of the belly board, and how tight the crawlspace is. If a seller gives you an R-value or a savings figure, ask for the manufacturer's written spec sheet for that exact panel, and treat any number without one as a sales line.

Two things matter more for comfort than the skirting does: an intact belly board and a working ground vapor barrier. Federal installation standards require a vapor retarder over the ground when the space under the home is enclosed with skirting, unless the home is in an arid region with dry soil ([24 CFR 3285.204](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-24/section-3285.204)). Marion County is not arid. If you are closing in the underside with new skirting and the ground is bare, fix that first.

## Ventilation and access still apply

Solid stone-look panels make it easy to forget the openings. The federal rules for a crawlspace with skirting do not change by material ([24 CFR 3285.505](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-24/section-3285.505)):

- Ventilation openings are sized by floor area, with a smaller total allowed when a ground vapor retarder covers the whole area under the home.
- Openings go on at least two opposite sides, as high as practicable.
- Each opening is covered with a perforated, corrosion-resistant covering that keeps rodents out.
- An access opening at least 18 inches wide and 24 inches high, and at least three square feet in area, must be placed so the utility connections under the home can be reached.
- Dryer vents and combustion air inlets must pass through the skirting to the outside.

Foam panels can be cut and fitted with vent inserts like any other skirting. The point is to count and place the vents before the panels go up, not after. Our guide to [skirting vents](/blog/mobile-home-skirting-vents/) walks through the math and the layout.

The access panel is the one owners forget. Faux stone panels are often fastened in a way that looks permanent. A hidden or screwed-shut access door means the first plumber, AC tech or inspector has to cut your skirting. Ask where it will go and how it opens.

## The track has to float with the home

A mobile home moves. Frost heave is rare in Florida, but settling is not: sandy soil compacts, piers sink, and heavy rain shifts the ground around footings.

Skirting that is fixed rigidly to both the ground and the home frame fights that movement. When a corner of the home drops half an inch, a rigid panel cracks or pops its fasteners. The usual way to avoid it is a bottom track or backer set at the ground and a top attachment that lets the panel slide or flex slightly, not a panel locked in place on all four edges. Follow the panel manufacturer's installation instructions for the exact method and fasteners, because they vary by product.

Practical points we check:

- The top of the panel clears the siding, so there is a gap to move into.
- The bottom track or backer sits on stable, compacted ground, not on loose sand piled against the home.
- Panels are not tied into the piers or anchors. Skirting never supports or hides a bad pier.
- There is room to relevel. If the home is likely to need it, the panels should come off and go back on without breaking.

## Termites, animals and inspection access

Solid skirting hides what is under the home. That includes wet insulation, a leaking drain line, mud tubes along a pier, and whatever moved in last month. A stone-look finish also makes a damaged section harder to spot from outside.

What we recommend, whichever material you choose:

- Keep the access panel usable and unobstructed. Do not stack storage in front of it.
- Look under the home at least once a year, and after any hard storm, flood or pest sighting.
- Keep wood, mulch and soil from touching the skirting or the home siding.
- If animals are already living under the home, get them out first. New skirting only seals them in.

If you have a termite treatment plan or warranty, ask the pest company whether foam skirting affects their inspection or coverage before you buy.

## Is faux stone skirting right for your home?

It makes sense when you want a stiffer, better-looking wall than vinyl, the underside is dry and sound, and you accept that "insulated" mostly means comfort, not a big drop in the power bill. It does not make sense if the home is out of level, the ground stays wet, or you are hoping the skirting will fix a structural problem.

Order of work matters: level first, then anchors, then ground moisture, then skirting. We cover the first two on our [leveling](/mobile-home-leveling-ocala/) and tie-down pages, and the skirting side on our [Ocala skirting page](/mobile-home-skirting-ocala/).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is faux stone skirting the same as real rock skirting?

No. Faux stone skirting is a lightweight panel, usually rigid foam or molded polymer, with a stone pattern on the face. Real stone or block is masonry on a footing, which is a foundation project, not a skirting panel. The look is similar from the street, but the cost, weight and permitting are very different.

### Does insulated skirting lower my electric bill in Florida?

It can slow the temperature swing under the home, but we do not promise a dollar saving. The result depends on your floor insulation, belly board, ducts and vapor barrier. If a seller quotes an R-value or savings figure, ask for the manufacturer's written spec for that exact panel.

### Do I still need vents and an access door with foam skirting?

Yes. Federal installation standards call for ventilation openings sized by floor area, placed on at least two opposite sides, and an access opening of at least 18 by 24 inches and three square feet, located so the utility connections can be reached. The material does not change those requirements.
