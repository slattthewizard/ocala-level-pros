A sloping mobile home floor is usually the frame sitting lower on one side or corner because a pier settled. Sometimes it is a dip between piers, or a soft spot where the subfloor has failed. The shape of the slope tells you which one. A floor that tilts the same way across the whole home is a different problem than one that sags in one room.

This guide shows how to measure the slope with tools you already own, how to read what you find, and which fix goes with which pattern. We do the level and the piers. We do not do subfloor carpentry, and we will say where that line falls.

## Measure First, Then Guess

Don't judge a floor by feel. People walk a gentle slope for years without noticing, and a bounce or a soft spot can feel like a slope when it is not one. Measure.

**A marble or a ball.** Set it on a hard floor and let go. If it rolls the same direction in several rooms, the home tilts that way. It will not tell you how much, but it finds the direction fast.

**A 4-foot level.** Lay it on the floor along the length of a room, then across. Slip a shim or a stack of cardboard under the low end until the bubble centers, then measure the gap. Do it in each room and write down the numbers.

**A string line.** Where you can see the floor edge, such as a bare room or inside a closet, stretch a taut string from one wall to the other a few inches above the floor. Measure the distance from string to floor at both ends and in the middle. A middle number that is larger than the ends means a dip. Smaller means a hump.

**A water level or laser.** A hose water level or a cheap line laser carries one height across the whole home. Take readings at the same spots in every room. Our post on [how pros check if a mobile home is level](/blog/how-pros-check-mobile-home-level/) goes through the tools and what each one is good for.

Sketch the floor plan and mark every reading. The pattern shows up on paper faster than it does in your head.

## Read the Pattern

Four patterns cover most sloping floors.

### The whole home tilts one way

Every reading drops steadily from one end or one side to the other. Doors and windows on the low side tend to bind, and the skirting may bulge or pull away at the high side.

That is a leveling problem. A line of piers or one whole side has settled, or the pads under them are sinking. The fix is a full relevel.

### One corner is low

Readings are fine over most of the home and fall off sharply near one corner. Look at the wall above that corner for cracks, and at the doors and windows nearby.

That usually points to one or two piers, or a single failing pad or footing. Heavy rain on sandy soil, a downspout dumping next to the pier, or a washed-out pad are common reasons. A single-corner relevel often covers it.

### A dip between piers

The floor is level at the piers and sags in the span between them. You may feel it as a gentle valley in the middle of a room rather than a tilt.

That can mean the beam or floor joists are flexing between supports, or a pier is missing or spaced too far from its neighbor. HUD's rule for adjacent supports is a good yardstick. [24 CFR 3285.6](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-24/section-3285.6) says a home will be considered adequately leveled if there is no more than 1/4 inch difference between adjacent pier supports (frame or perimeter) and the exterior doors and windows do not bind and can be properly operated. If the piers are within that and the floor still dips, look at what spans between them. Our post on [bouncy floors in a mobile home](/blog/bouncy-floors-mobile-home/) covers the span and joist side of the problem.

### A soft spot or a localized sag

The floor gives underfoot in one place, feels spongy, or the covering has a hollow sound. That is a failed subfloor, not a settled home. Water from a leak, a bad seal, or a torn belly board rots the subfloor from the underside or the top.

Press the spot with your hand. If it flexes, it is likely damaged. Our post on [soft spots in a mobile home floor](/blog/soft-spots-mobile-home-floor/) covers how to find them and what causes them.

## Which Fix Goes With Which Pattern

| What you see | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Whole home tilts one way | Settled piers or pads along one side or end | Full relevel |
| One corner low | One or two failed piers or pads | Single-corner relevel, reset or replace those piers |
| Dip between piers | Flexing beam or joists, or a pier missing or too far from its neighbor | Add or reset pier support; check the frame |
| Soft spot or spongy area | Rotted or water-damaged subfloor | Subfloor repair, plus fixing the water source |
| Slope plus soft floor | Both | Relevel first, then repair the subfloor |

Some homes show two patterns at once. Wet soil can sink a pad while a leak soaks the floor above it.

## What Settling Looks Like Elsewhere in the Home

A floor rarely slopes alone. Look for the companions.

- Doors that stick or won't latch. See our post on [mobile home doors that won't close](/blog/mobile-home-doors-wont-close/).
- Windows that bind or show uneven gaps.
- Cracks in walls and ceilings, mostly at door and window corners.
- Gaps between the wall and the floor, or baseboards pulling away.
- Skirting that buckles or pulls away on one side.

Two or three of these together with a measured slope is settling. A slope with none of them, and a bounce or soft feel in one spot, leans toward floor damage.

## Why the Floor Slopes in Marion County

Marion County is sandy, and sand drains fast but does not hold shape under load. Heavy summer rain washes fines out from under pier pads, pads tilt, and the pier above them drops. In low spots, water that stays under the home keeps the ground soft. Shims dry out and slide. A leaking drain line or a torn belly can keep one area wet for months.

None of those need a dramatic event. The slope can creep in over a few seasons, and many owners only notice after a door starts to stick. Our post on [why mobile homes settle in Florida](/blog/why-mobile-homes-settle-florida/) explains the ground side in more detail.

## What You Can Do Before Anyone Shows Up

- Measure and sketch the floor, and take dated photos.
- Check that gutters and downspouts drain away from the home, and that the ground slopes away from the skirting.
- Look for standing water or damp soil along the skirting after rain.
- Don't stuff cardboard or scrap lumber under the home to fix a slope. It compresses and rots.
- Don't cut or shim doors to hide the problem. That makes the next relevel harder.

If you crawl under the home to look, never get under a home supported only by a jack. A jack is for lifting, not for holding weight over a person.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much slope is normal in a mobile home floor?

HUD's installation rule treats a home as adequately leveled when there is no more than 1/4 inch difference between adjacent pier supports and the exterior doors and windows do not bind. A floor that slopes enough to roll a marble across several rooms is worth measuring.

### Is a sloping floor in a mobile home dangerous?

A mild slope is mostly a nuisance. It can still strain the frame, the walls, and the plumbing over time, so it is worth a look. A slope with a soft or spongy area is more urgent, because the subfloor may be failing.

### Can I fix an uneven floor by adding shims under the home?

Shims are part of a relevel, but it takes lifting the frame with jacks and checking every pier, and it is not a safe job to improvise. Never get under a home supported only by a jack. Loose scrap lumber and stacked cardboard compress and rot, so they are not a fix.

### Do you repair the subfloor if it is rotted?

No. We level homes, handle piers, tie-downs, vapor barriers, and skirting. If the subfloor is damaged, a floor repair contractor handles that. We can level the home first so the repair stays put, and we can fix the moisture source underneath if it is a ground barrier problem.

### Will releveling fix a sloping floor?

If the slope comes from settled piers or pads, yes. A relevel brings the frame back to level, and a floor that tilts because of settling will flatten with it. A dip between piers or a soft spot needs pier support or subfloor work in addition.
