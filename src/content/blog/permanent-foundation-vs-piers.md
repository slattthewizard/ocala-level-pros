## What Counts as a Permanent Foundation on a Mobile Home?

The term gets thrown around loosely, which causes a lot of confusion. A true permanent foundation for a manufactured home has to meet the standards in HUD's Permanent Foundations Guide for Manufactured Housing. You can read the official guide on [HUD's website](https://www.hud.gov/program_offices/housing/sfh/model/pfgmh).

In plain terms, a permanent foundation means:

- The home's wheels, axles, and tow hitch are removed.
- The home rests on footings that extend below grade and are built from durable materials like poured concrete or mortared masonry.
- The foundation resists wind, flood, and seismic forces, not just gravity.
- The home is anchored to that foundation, and the perimeter is enclosed with a continuous wall or engineered skirting system.

Common versions include a full poured perimeter wall with interior piers, a concrete slab with the home attached, or a retrofit system of engineered brackets and mortared block that an engineer will certify. That last part matters. For FHA, VA, or conventional financing, a licensed engineer usually has to inspect the foundation and issue a certification letter stating it meets HUD standards. We cover that whole process in our guide to [FHA foundation certification for mobile homes](/blog/fha-foundation-certification-mobile-home/).

What a permanent foundation is not: dry-stacked concrete block piers with steel straps to ground anchors, no matter how well built. That's a pier and anchor system, and it's what the vast majority of mobile homes in Marion County sit on today.

## How Pier and Anchor Systems Work in Florida

A standard Florida setup uses concrete block piers spaced along the steel frame, capped with wood or ABS plastic pads and shimmed tight against the beams. Steel anchors screwed into the ground connect to the frame with galvanized straps, which is what keeps the home in place when the wind picks up.

Done right, this system is legitimate, code-compliant, and insurable. Florida has some of the strictest tie-down rules in the country, and a properly installed pier and anchor setup passes 4-point inspections and wind mitigation reviews all day long. If your straps and anchors are aging, our [mobile home tie-down service in Ocala](/mobile-home-tie-downs-ocala/) can bring the whole system up to current code.

The tradeoff is maintenance. Piers sit on soil, and Central Florida soil moves. Sandy stretches around Silver Springs and Citra drain fast and shift after heavy rain. Clay pockets near Dunnellon swell and shrink with moisture. Over the years, piers settle unevenly, homes drift out of level, and you start noticing [doors that won't close](/blog/mobile-home-doors-wont-close/) or cracks creeping across interior walls.

That's not a failure of the pier concept. It's just the deal you accept: lower cost up front, periodic releveling later. Most homes here need a relevel every 3 to 5 years, and the job typically runs $450 to $950 for a single wide and $700 to $1,500 for a double wide through our [mobile home leveling service in Ocala](/mobile-home-leveling-ocala/).

## When a Permanent Foundation Makes Sense

There are real situations where the bigger investment is the right call.

**You need FHA, VA, or USDA financing to sell.** These loan programs require the home to be on a HUD-compliant permanent foundation with an engineer's certification. If your likely buyer pool is using government-backed loans, a home on piers alone shrinks your market. In some parts of Marion County, that's most first-time buyers.

**You want the home classified as real property.** In Florida, a mobile home on land you own can be converted from personal property to real property. You retire the title through the state, and the home gets taxed with the land instead of carrying an annual decal. That conversion generally requires the home to be permanently affixed. Real property status can improve resale value and opens up standard mortgage products.

**You're planning additions or major renovations.** Porches, carports, and room additions attach better and permit easier when the main structure sits on a permanent foundation. Some Marion County permit reviews go smoother when the foundation question is already settled.

**You own the land and plan to stay 15+ years.** Long ownership horizons let the upfront cost spread out, and you get decades of not thinking about releveling.

James, who owns a place off Highway 42 in Summerfield, fit this profile. He'd bought his lot outright, planned to retire there, and wanted to add a 12x24 screened room. He spent $22,000 on an engineered retrofit foundation with certification. Two years later he refinanced into a conventional mortgage at a rate his old chattel loan couldn't touch, and the monthly savings started chipping away at that $22,000 immediately.

## When Piers Are the Smarter Choice

For most of the homes we work on, staying with a well-maintained pier and anchor system is the right answer. Here's when.

**You rent your lot.** This one is almost automatic. If the home sits in a park or on leased land, a permanent foundation ties your investment to ground you don't own. Nearly every park home in our service area stays on piers, and that's correct.

**The home is older or lower value.** Putting a $30,000 foundation under a home worth $35,000 rarely makes financial sense. That money does more good on a solid relevel, new tie-downs, a fresh vapor barrier, and subfloor repairs.

**You might move the home.** It happens more than people think. Family land changes hands, parks close, opportunities come up. A home on piers can be moved. A home on a permanent foundation effectively can't, at least not without losing the foundation investment entirely.

**Cash flow matters more than loan flexibility.** A complete pier and tie-down refresh costs a fraction of a conversion and gets you a safe, code-compliant, insurable home right now. You can always convert later if a sale or refinance demands it.

**Your current system just needs attention, not replacement.** A lot of homeowners assume they need something drastic when the real issue is a handful of settled or crumbling piers. ## What Florida Weather and Insurance Add to the Decision

Living in Central Florida adds two practical wrinkles: wind and water.

On wind, the honest answer is that both systems can perform well when they're built and maintained to code. Florida homes in our area fall under specific wind zone requirements, and the anchoring, whether straps to ground anchors or bolts to a concrete wall, is what does the work in a storm. A neglected permanent foundation with corroded connections is worse than a fresh, certified tie-down system. FEMA publishes solid building science guidance on manufactured home anchoring and flood protection at [fema.gov](https://www.fema.gov/emergency-managers/risk-management/building-science).

On insurance, your carrier cares about documentation. A 4-point inspection will look at how the home is supported and anchored either way. Homes with current tie-down certifications or engineer foundation letters tend to have an easier time at renewal. If you've had a policy question flagged, our article on [tie-down certification and insurance](/blog/tie-down-certification-insurance/) explains what inspectors are looking for.

On water, elevation matters. Pier systems naturally hold the home 24 to 36 inches off grade, which keeps the frame and belly out of standing water and leaves room for airflow and inspections. Permanent foundations with enclosed perimeters need proper vents and drainage planning so moisture doesn't get trapped underneath. Either way, standing water under a mobile home is a problem worth fixing fast.

One safety note before you grab a jack and crawl under there yourself. Checking your skirting or looking for leaning piers with a flashlight is fine. Lifting a home, moving piers, or adjusting anchors is not a DIY job. Homes weigh 15,000 to 30,000 pounds, and a slipped jack under that load is how people get killed. When it's time to touch the support system, call a pro.

So how do you actually decide? Boil it down to four questions:

1. **Do you own the land?** No means piers, almost every time.
2. **Will you or your buyer need FHA, VA, or USDA financing?** Yes pushes you toward a certified permanent foundation.
3. **Is the home worth at least 2 to 3 times the conversion cost?** If not, invest in the pier system instead.
4. **Are you staying 15+ years on land you own?** If yes and the budget allows, a permanent foundation starts to earn its keep.

If you land somewhere in the middle, start with an inspection. Knowing whether your current setup needs $800 of work or $8,000 of work changes the math on everything else.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is a permanent foundation required for a mobile home in Florida?

No. Florida requires proper support and tie-downs that meet state anchoring rules, but a code-compliant pier and anchor system is fully legal. Permanent foundations only become a requirement when a specific loan program, like FHA or VA, or a real property conversion demands one.

### How much does it cost to put a mobile home on a permanent foundation?

In the Ocala area, expect roughly $15,000 to $45,000 or more depending on the method. Engineered retrofits on well-sited homes land at the low end, while full perimeter stem walls with a lift and reset land at the high end. Add $400 to $700 for the engineer's certification letter most lenders require.

### Does a permanent foundation increase a mobile home's value?

It usually does, mainly by widening the buyer pool to include FHA, VA, and conventional borrowers and by allowing real property classification. Whether the value gain exceeds the cost depends on the home's age, the land, and your local market. On older or lower-value homes, the math often doesn't work.

### Do mobile homes on permanent foundations still need leveling?

Rarely, which is one of their main perks. A properly built foundation with footings below grade resists the settling that pier systems experience. That said, no foundation is immune to serious soil problems, so sloping floors or sticking doors on any home deserve a professional look.
